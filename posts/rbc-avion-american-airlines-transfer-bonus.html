RBC Avion Is Offering a 15% Transfer Bonus to American Airlines AAdvantage

If you are sitting on a pile of RBC Avion Rewards points and you have had your eye on an American Airlines or oneworld partner flight, there is a limited-time reason to pay attention this month. RBC has brought back its annual summer promotion: a 15% bonus when Avion Elite members convert Avion points into American Airlines AAdvantage miles. It is one of the simpler ways for Canadians to feed a program that quietly punches above its weight for premium cabin travel.

The offer runs from August 4 to September 3, 2026, which lines up almost exactly with a separate Amex promotion we will come back to further down. First, let us break down what the Avion bonus actually gets you.

Header Image Source: American Airlines

The 15% Bonus, Explained

Source: RBC Avion

Normally, Avion points convert to American Airlines AAdvantage miles at a rate of 10 Avion points for 7 AAdvantage miles, or 0.7 miles per point. During this promotion, that jumps to 10 Avion points for 8.05 AAdvantage miles, which works out to 0.805 miles per point. That is the 15% uplift.

Here is what that looks like across a few transfer amounts:

  • 5,000 Avion points become 4,025 AAdvantage miles (versus 3,500 at the standard rate)
  • 10,000 Avion points become 8,050 AAdvantage miles
  • 60,000 Avion points become 48,300 AAdvantage miles
  • 75,000 Avion points become roughly 60,375 AAdvantage miles

That last line is worth remembering, because as you will see in the real-life examples below, around 60,000 AAdvantage miles is a one-way business class seat to Japan or Finland on a oneworld partner such as Japan Airlines or Finnair.

Who Qualifies

This is an Avion Elite offer, so you need to hold an eligible RBC Avion credit card. The consumer cards that qualify are the RBC Avion Visa Infinite, the RBC Visa Platinum Avion, and the RBC Avion Visa Infinite Privilege, and the business and commercial Avion cards are included as well. You also need to be enrolled in the American Airlines AAdvantage program, and your AAdvantage account has to be in the same name as your Avion account, otherwise the transfer can stall.

How to Grab It

Source: RBC Avion

  1. If you are not already an AAdvantage member, enrol for free at aa.com/aadvantage.
  2. Sign in to RBC, choose "Get AAdvantage miles," and convert a minimum of 5,000 Avion points before the deadline. You can also do this by phone with RBC if you prefer.
  3. Once the miles land, book your flight through American Airlines.

A few details to keep in mind. RBC says to allow up to four weeks for the miles to appear in your AAdvantage account, so this is not an instant transfer. The minimum to trigger the bonus is 5,000 points, and the conversion is permanent, so there is no path back to Avion once the points move. One more thing that catches people out: miles earned through a promotion like this do not count as Loyalty Points toward AAdvantage elite status, so treat these purely as currency for booking flights.

Stack It With the Amex to Marriott Bonus

Source: American Express

Running at the same time is a promotion I recently covered: American Express Canada is offering a 30% bonus when you transfer Membership Rewards points to Marriott Bonvoy, also live until September 3, 2026. On its own that is a hotel play, but there is a reason it belongs in the same conversation as the Avion offer.

Marriott Bonvoy transfers to American Airlines AAdvantage at a rate of 3 Marriott points to 1 AAdvantage mile. That matters because Membership Rewards points cannot be sent to AAdvantage directly here in Canada. Routing through Marriott is the workaround, and it opens a second pipeline into the same AAdvantage balance you are topping up with Avion.

Here is the honest math, though. With the 30% bonus, 1,000 Membership Rewards points become 1,560 Marriott points. At 3:1, those 1,560 Marriott points become 520 AAdvantage miles. So the effective rate lands around 1,000 Membership Rewards points for 520 AAdvantage miles.

Two caveats keep this in perspective. First, AAdvantage is one of the airline programs excluded from Marriott's standing bonus of 5,000 extra miles per 60,000 points transferred, so the "transfer in 60,000-point chunks" trick that points people love does not apply here. It is a flat 3:1. Second, Membership Rewards points usually stretch further sent straight to programs like Aeroplan and Air France KLM Flying Blue for a premium cabin seat. So think of the Marriott route as a way to top up an AAdvantage balance you are a little short on for a specific booking, not as your main strategy.

What Canadians Can Actually Do With AAdvantage Miles

Source: oneworld

The reason AAdvantage is worth building up comes down to the oneworld alliance and one quirk of how American prices its awards. Flights on American's own aircraft use dynamic pricing, so the mileage moves around with demand. Award flights on American's oneworld partners, though, follow a fixed published chart. For premium cabins, those partner awards are usually both cheaper and more predictable than booking American metal, which is exactly what you want when you are spending miles on a lie-flat seat.

A quick comparison makes the point. On American's own flights, a one-way business class seat to Europe starts at around 75,000 miles and a seat to Asia starts at around 95,000 miles, and both can climb from there depending on the date. Book the same regions on a oneworld partner and the fixed chart takes over.

Source: American Airlines

Here are the partner sweet spots that matter most for Canadians, all one-way from Canada:

  • Business class to Europe: 57,500 miles. A lie-flat seat to London, Helsinki, or Madrid on British Airways, Finnair, or Iberia.
  • Business class to Asia: 60,000 miles for Asia Region 1, which covers Japan, South Korea, and Hong Kong. This is the one that lines up with roughly 75,000 Avion points after the 15% bonus.
  • Qatar Airways Qsuites to the Middle East: 70,000 miles, on one of the most decorated business class products in the world.

Because Canada sits in the same award region as the contiguous United States on American's chart, you can start these itineraries right here at home. Two things to watch. If your routing includes a lie-flat connecting flight within North America, American adds 7,500 miles to the business level. And carrier surcharges vary by partner, so confirm the full taxes and fees on aa.com before you transfer, because British Airways and Iberia in particular can add a meaningful cash amount on top.

A First Example: Vancouver to Tokyo

Say you want to fly Vancouver to Tokyo in business class on Japan Airlines. On the partner chart, that nonstop comes in at 60,000 AAdvantage miles one-way, plus just $60.67 CAD in taxes and fees. Because it is a nonstop with no connection inside North America, there is no lie-flat surcharge to worry about, so it stays right at the 60,000 level. To get there through this promotion, you would convert roughly 75,000 Avion points, which at the boosted 0.805 rate produces just over 60,000 miles.

Now look at the cash price. Book that exact nonstop directly with Japan Airlines and it runs about $4,100 CAD one-way. So the choice is 60,000 miles plus $60.67 CAD in taxes, or roughly $4,100 CAD out of pocket. That works out to close to 6.7 cents of value for every AAdvantage mile (CAD), and because you only needed around 75,000 Avion points to create those miles, each Avion point is pulling its weight at roughly 5.4 cents. That is several times what Avion points typically return on an everyday redemption, and it is the whole reason a targeted transfer like this is worth doing.

One note on how I reach that value. Normally, when I judge a one-way award, I compare it against a round-trip cash fare rather than a one-way, because one-way long-haul cash prices are usually wildly inflated and make an award look better than it really is. This route was the exception. The round-trip cash fare was ballooning toward $12,000 CAD, which is not a realistic benchmark, while the one-way sat at a sensible $4,100 CAD. So here the one-way cash price was the fair comparison.

A Second Example: Toronto to Helsinki

The same logic works to Europe, and here the usual rule about one-way fares applies. Say you want to fly Toronto to Helsinki in business class on Finnair, a oneworld partner. On the partner chart that nonstop is 57,500 AAdvantage miles one-way, plus $79.62 CAD in taxes. Notice how low that fee is. Finnair does not pass big surcharges through AAdvantage, unlike British Airways or Iberia on the same route.

This time the one-way cash fare is the inflated one, sitting north of $10,000 CAD, so it is useless as a yardstick. The realistic number is the round-trip business fare of about $4,935 CAD. The outbound alone books for 57,500 AAdvantage miles plus $79.62 CAD in taxes, so measuring that one-way award against the full round-trip cash fare, the miles are returning around 8.4 cents each (CAD), or roughly 6.8 cents per Avion point once the transfer bonus is factored in. That is another strong result, and it puts a premium transatlantic business seat within reach for a manageable pile of points.

My Thoughts

I have always liked AAdvantage as a quiet second string to Aeroplan. Aeroplan is my go-to for Star Alliance premium seats, but it does not get me onto Cathay Pacific, Japan Airlines, or Qatar Airways, and those are exactly the airlines I want when I am flying the long way to Asia. AAdvantage fills that gap through oneworld, and this Avion bonus is one of the cleaner ways for a Canadian to build a usable balance.

What I like about the timing is that the Avion and Amex promotions overlap, so if you are short on miles for a specific booking, you have two ways to close the gap. That said, the rule I hold myself to has not changed. I do not transfer a single point until I know exactly what I am booking.

The moment Avion points or Membership Rewards points move into AAdvantage, all of their flexibility is gone, and there is no reversing it. Speculatively converting and hoping to use the miles later is exactly how people end up with a stranded balance.

The Bottom Line

The 15% Avion to AAdvantage bonus is a strong offer, but only if you already know how you want to use the miles. Paired with the Amex to Marriott bonus, you have two routes into AAdvantage and a real shot at a premium cabin oneworld seat that Aeroplan simply cannot book for you.

Before you commit, do your homework on where those points are best spent. Avion Elite members also have other options, such as British Airways Club, which transfers 1:1 and runs its own periodic 30% bonuses, and can also be used across oneworld including American Airlines flights. But if AAdvantage is where your redemption lives, this bonus gives you more to work with.

Both promotions run until September 3, 2026, and remember that every one of these transfers is permanent, so only move your points once you have a plan.

Happy travels!
