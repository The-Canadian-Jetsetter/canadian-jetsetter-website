Porter Airlines Lands in London, Ontario: Winter Sun, Cruise Connections, and Another Way to Skip Pearson

If you live anywhere in Southwestern Ontario, your list of easy departure airports just got longer. Porter Airlines is bringing flights to London International Airport (YXU) for the first time in the airline's history. Starting this November, YXU will see four nonstop seasonal routes to warm-weather destinations in Florida, Mexico, and Jamaica, and the airline has confirmed that a full year-round domestic network is on the way in summer 2027.

For a region that has spent years watching residents drive elsewhere to catch a flight, this is a real shift. Here is what it means for you.

Header Image Source: Porter.

The Four Winter Routes

All four routes launch in late 2026 and run as seasonal winter service. Here is the schedule as announced:

Porter winter routes from London (YXU)
Route Start Date Peak Days
London (YXU) to Fort Lauderdale (FLL) November 18 4x weekly (Mon, Wed, Fri, Sun)
London (YXU) to Orlando (MCO) November 19 3x weekly (Tue, Thu, Sat)
London (YXU) to Cancun (CUN) December 17 4x weekly (Tue, Thu, Sat, Sun)
London (YXU) to Montego Bay (MBJ) December 18 2x weekly (Mon, Fri)

Flights are on sale now at flyporter.com and through travel agents.

Why This Matters for Southwestern Ontario

Source: Expedia.

For anyone in the London area, the standard advice for years has been simple: if you want options, drive to Toronto. This announcement changes the math.

London says roughly two million people live within about an hour's drive of YXU, and that is a lot of travellers who no longer have to trek to Pearson, pay for a week of Toronto parking, or sit in 401 traffic just to start a vacation. For a good chunk of Southwestern Ontario, YXU is closer, calmer, and far less of a headache than a big hub. When a smaller airport picks up real routes, it usually means less time getting to the gate and less stress on both ends of the trip. That is a win even before you look at the fares.

The Cruise Angle Nobody Is Talking About

Two of these four destinations are not just beach trips. They are cruise gateways.

Fort Lauderdale is home to Port Everglades, one of the busiest cruise ports in the world, and Orlando sits a short drive from Port Canaveral, another major embarkation point. If you have ever wanted to pair a Caribbean sailing with a nonstop flight from close to home, these routes open that up in a way that did not exist for London-area travellers before. Instead of connecting through a hub and hoping your bags keep up, you could fly direct into your cruise city the day before you sail. For anyone in the region who cruises, this is quietly one of the best parts of the announcement.

Porter's Strategy

Source: Porter.

There is a bigger story here about how Porter is choosing to grow. While Air Canada builds around its major hubs in Toronto, Montreal, and Vancouver, and WestJet leans on Calgary, Toronto, and Vancouver, Porter is heading in a different direction and going after smaller Canadian cities for its growth. Its own hubs at Toronto Billy Bishop, Toronto Pearson, and Ottawa sit close together, but the approach appears to be working, because Porter keeps expanding from them.

Ottawa is a good example. There is a lot of room to grow there, and travellers who are used to connecting through Montreal or Toronto find real relief in having direct flights instead. Now smaller cities like London are getting a taste of the same thing, paired with Porter's elevated economy product on the Embraer E195-E2, which has no middle seat anywhere in the cabin. I am hoping this strategy keeps paying off for Porter and that people respond well to the new routes, because more direct options for Canadians means more competition, lower prices, and more chances to travel.

A Domestic Network Is Coming in 2027

Source: Porter.

The winter sun routes are only the opening act. Porter has said it is committed to building a year-round domestic network out of London by summer 2027, connecting the city to destinations across Canada. Specific routes and schedules have not been announced yet, but this is the piece I will be watching most closely. Flights to southern destinations are great, but a permanent domestic base at YXU would change how the region connects to the rest of the country, and it would give Southwestern Ontario a home carrier for far more than a winter getaway.

My Thoughts

Regional airports getting real service is good for everyone: more competition, more convenience, and less pressure to funnel every single trip through Pearson. The destinations are exactly what a London-area traveller wants in the depths of an Ontario winter, and the fact that Porter is betting on a city this size says something about where the airline sees its future.

The cruise connection is the sleeper here. If you have family in the region who sail out of Florida, or you have been eyeing a Caribbean cruise yourself, a nonstop from London into Fort Lauderdale or Orlando makes the whole trip dramatically easier.

My only caution is the usual one: seasonal routes live and die by demand. If you want these flights to stick around, the best thing you can do is book them. I would also wait to get too excited about the domestic network until we see actual routes, because "coming in 2027" and "confirmed and bookable" are two different things.

The Bottom Line

Porter arriving at London International is a real upgrade for Southwestern Ontario.

Four nonstop winter routes to Fort Lauderdale, Orlando, Cancun, and Montego Bay start rolling out in November, two of them double as cruise gateways, and a year-round domestic network is promised for summer 2027. Flights are on sale now at flyporter.com. If a smaller, closer, less chaotic airport sounds appealing, and you have winter sun or a cruise on your radar, this one is worth a look.

Happy travels!
