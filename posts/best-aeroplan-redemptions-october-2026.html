Get the Most Out of Aeroplan's Points Promos (3 Example Itineraries)

Aeroplan is running a buy points promotion right now, offering up to a 90% bonus when you purchase points. The offer runs until October 29, 2026 at 11:59 PM PT, and I broke down the full promo and every bonus tier in a separate post you can read here.

In this post I want to do something more fun. A pile of points is only as good as what you can book with it, so I pulled together three standout Aeroplan redemptions worth your attention while this promo is live. For each one, I worked out exactly what it would cost to buy the points you need during the promo, then compared that to simply paying cash for the same flights.

One important note on how I am running the numbers: for every example below, I am assuming you are starting from zero Aeroplan points and buying the entire itinerary's worth. I did this to keep the comparison honest and easy to follow. In reality, most of you reading this already have at least a small balance sitting in your account, so you will likely only need to top up a little rather than buy the full amount. That makes every one of these deals even better than the numbers show.

A couple of quick reminders before we dive in. You also pay sales tax on top of the purchase price, which varies by province, so treat the buy prices below as "plus tax." Award pricing and seat availability also move constantly, so these are snapshots of what I found on the day.

1. Calgary to Denver in Economy, Last Minute

This one is a great hack if you need to fly into the United States last minute.

Under Aeroplan's Within North America award chart, United is a Select Partner, so technically its flights are priced dynamically, like Air Canada's own. In practice, though, they tend to stay fairly static.

For a round trip from Calgary to Denver departing October 22 and returning October 28, booked just a day in advance, the cash fare had climbed dramatically to $1,356 CAD. On points it was 15,000 each way, so 30,000 points round trip plus the taxes and fees.

For this route, 15,000 is the points price I see almost all the time, and it makes the most sense to take advantage of it for last-minute bookings, since that is when United starts raising its cash fares heavily.

Cash fare (round trip, economy) $1,356 CAD
Points route 30,000 points + $239 in taxes and fees ($159 outbound, $80 inbound)
Cost to buy the points in the promo 20,000 base + 50% bonus = 30,000 points for $750 CAD (before tax)
Total to book with points $750 (points) + $239 (taxes and fees) = about $989, plus sales tax on the purchase
You save about $1,356 vs $989, roughly $367 CAD before purchase tax

Point value: about 3.72 cents per point, bought in at 2.5 cents.

That works out to a redemption value of 3.72 cents per point, and since you are buying the points at 2.5 cents in the 50% tier, you come out ahead even acquiring every point from scratch.

This is a strong result for an economy ticket. The points price holds steady at 15,000 while a last-minute cash fare climbs, and that gap is exactly what makes buying in worth it. On a cheap advance fare the same route would not be worth the points, so save this one for when you need to fly on short notice.

2. Winnipeg to Churchill, the Polar Bear Capital

Remote northern routes are some of the best value in the entire Aeroplan program, because the cash fares tend to be very high. Churchill, Manitoba is the polar bear capital of the world, and getting there has never been cheap.

For a round trip running February 1 to 5, 2027, the cash fare came in at $1,914 CAD. On points it was 15,000 each way, so 30,000 points round trip, with very low taxes and fees attached.

Cash fare (round trip) $1,914 CAD
Points route 30,000 points + $118 in taxes and fees ($79 outbound, $39 inbound)
Cost to buy the points in the promo 20,000 base + 50% bonus = 30,000 points for $750 CAD (before tax)
Total to book with points $750 (points) + $118 (taxes and fees) = about $868, plus sales tax on the purchase
You save about $1,914 vs $868, roughly $1,046 CAD before purchase tax

Point value: about 5.99 cents per point, bought in at 2.5 cents.

The exact same 30,000 points as the Denver example, bought for the exact same $750, but here they wipe out a far pricier cash fare. The redemption value lands at a huge 5.99 cents per point, which is nearly three times what you pay for the points in the promo.

This is the remote-route magic in a nutshell, and it is why northern Canada is one of the first places I look when a buy points promo comes around.

3. Montreal to Zurich in SWISS Business Class

Now for the showstopper. This is the trip you could never justify paying cash for, made attainable by a points purchase. A lie-flat SWISS business class seat from Montreal to Zurich and back, departing November 4 and returning November 19.

The cash fare for this round trip in business was a staggering $9,979 CAD. On points it was 60,000 each way, so 120,000 points round trip plus taxes and fees. Take note that SWISS is an Aeroplan partner, so their redemption values are fixed, which is great for us consumers!

Cash fare (round trip, business) $9,979 CAD
Points route 120,000 points + $211 in taxes and fees ($120 outbound, $91 inbound)
Cost to buy the points in the promo 70,000 base + 75% bonus = 122,500 points for $2,625 CAD (before tax). That is slightly more than you need, leaving about 2,500 points left over
Total to book with points $2,625 (points) + $211 (taxes and fees) = about $2,836, plus sales tax on the purchase
You save about $9,979 vs $2,836, roughly $7,143 CAD before purchase tax

Point value: about 8.14 cents per point, bought in at 2.14 cents.

This is where buying points goes from smart to spectacular. The redemption value comes out to 8.14 cents per point, while the 75% tier lets you buy in at just 2.14 cents. That is close to a four times return on every point.

You are turning roughly $2,836 into a business class experience that would otherwise cost nearly $10,000. For a bucket-list trip in a lie-flat seat, I can think of very few better uses of a buy points promo.

One small planning note. Buying 70,000 base points is the right move here, since it lands you in the 75% tier and gives you 122,500 points, just over the 120,000 you need. Reaching for the 90% tier would force you to buy 80,000 base points and 152,000 total, which is more money for points this trip does not require. Match your purchase to your target.

My Thoughts

What I love about these three is how they show the full spectrum.

A last-minute economy hop, a remote domestic gem, and an aspirational business class seat, all unlocked by the same cheap points. The pattern is always the same: buy points low during a promo, then point them at a redemption where the cash price is high. That gap is where the value lives.

Something I should emphasize, though, is to not buy points without a plan. You should always have an itinerary you are working toward, or at least an idea of a trip you want to book. Buying points with no plan is not something I recommend, because you could lock yourself into Aeroplan when, depending on the trip, it might have been more beneficial to purchase points with another loyalty program.

The Bottom Line

The buy points promo runs until October 29, 2026, so the window to top up is short. If one of these redemptions is calling your name, or you have your own trip in mind, now is a reasonable time to buy the points you need.

As always, my one rule on buying points holds: only buy them if you have a specific redemption or a near-term trip in mind. Points bought to sit in your account with no plan rarely pay off, and award prices and seat availability can change between now and when you book.

But when you have the trip picked out and the cash fare is high, a promo like this turns a good redemption into a great one.

Happy travels!
