The Toronto to London Business Class Deal You Should Not Book the Obvious Way

If you have been waiting for a reliable, low-cost way to fly fully lie-flat business class between Toronto and London, this is one of the best options available to Canadians right now.

There is one small but important catch: the flight is operated by Virgin Atlantic, but you do not want to book it through Virgin Atlantic. Here is how it works.

The Deal at a Glance

Source: Virgin Atlantic.

Right now there is a lot of award availability on the nonstop between Toronto (YYZ) and London Heathrow (LHR) in Virgin Atlantic business class for 69,500 miles each way, plus taxes and fees. Both directions are direct, and you fly on a fully lie-flat business class seat in a 1-2-1 configuration on their Airbus A350.

Outbound, YYZ to LHR: departs 22:45, arrives 10:50 the next day (7h05)

Toronto to London Heathrow Outbound: 69,500 Flying Blue Miles + fees

Return, LHR to YYZ: departs 17:25, arrives 20:15 (7h50)

London Heathrow to Toronto Inbound: 69,500 Flying Blue Miles + fees

The fees are where the two directions differ. The outbound out of Toronto carries about $368.92 CAD in taxes and fees. The return out of Heathrow carries about $980.30 CAD, largely because of the United Kingdom's high departure tax. That difference matters, and I will come back to it below.

Why You Book Through Air France KLM Flying Blue, Not Virgin Atlantic

This is the part that saves you a headache. While the flight is on Virgin Atlantic metal, the smart move is to search for and book it through Air France KLM using its Flying Blue program.

The reason is simple. American Express Canada Membership Rewards points transfer to Air France KLM Flying Blue at a one-to-one rate. That is a far easier and more direct path than trying to move your points over to Virgin Atlantic. Same seat, same flight, far less friction on the way in.

Transferring AMEX MR to Flying Blue Miles

If you are short on Membership Rewards, you can top up Flying Blue through Marriott Bonvoy, which transfers at three-to-one and adds 5,000 bonus miles for every 60,000 points you move. That works out to 25,000 Flying Blue miles per 60,000 Bonvoy points, an effective rate of about 2.4 to one.

What You Get on Board

Source: Virgin Atlantic.

Booked as a Flying Blue business fare, each passenger receives:

  • One small bag (40 x 30 x 15 cm)
  • Two hand baggage items (55 x 35 x 25 cm)
  • Two checked bags (32 kg each)
  • Free standard seat selection at check-in
  • Lounge access
  • SkyPriority benefits
  • A changeable ticket (EUR 70 fee plus any fare difference)
  • A refundable ticket (EUR 70 fee if you cancel before the first flight in your trip)

Is It Good Value?

Source: Virgin Atlantic.

Yes, and the honest way to measure it is round trip against round trip.

A full round trip costs 139,000 miles plus roughly $1,349 CAD in combined fees. The comparable round-trip cash fare in business class comes in at $4,161 CAD. After subtracting the fees you pay on the award ticket, that works out to 2.02 cents per Flying Blue mile. For a lie-flat business class seat, that is good value on its own.

Toronto to London Heathrow Round-Trip Cash Price: $4,161

It is worth noting that the return direction is what drags the average down, because of those high Heathrow departure taxes. If you look at the outbound alone, the picture is even stronger.

Following my usual approach of comparing a one-way redemption against a round-trip cash fare (since one-way cash tickets tend to be inflated, as this route shows), the outbound at 69,500 miles plus $368.92 CAD, measured against that $4,161 CAD round-trip fare, returns 5.46 cents per mile.

For the record, the one-way cash fare on the same outbound is $5,574 CAD, which is more expensive than the entire round trip. Measured against that inflated number, the outbound would show 7.49 cents per mile. I do not lead with that figure, because a one-way cash fare that costs more than a round trip is not a fair benchmark. I mention it only so you can see just how skewed one-way pricing gets on this route.

Toronto to London Heathrow One-Way Cash Price: $5,574

The takeaway: even at the conservative 2.02 cents per mile for a full round trip, this is a strong redemption. If you only need the outbound, the value climbs meaningfully.

A Way to Squeeze Out Even More Value

Since the fees are the only real drag on this redemption, there is a way to push the value higher. The idea is to fly into London Heathrow on Virgin Atlantic, where the outbound fees are low, and then book your return home from a different European country using a program with lower taxes and surcharges. Countries such as Spain and Ireland are known for lower departure costs.

Here is a worked example. Iberia flies nonstop from Madrid to Toronto, and its off-peak business class award pricing in Avios is a sweet-spot. On the date I checked, that seat was available at the off-peak business rate, with taxes and fees well below what Heathrow charges on the way out.

Madrid to Toronto One-Way: 40,500 Avios + fees

The off-peak business seat prices at 40,500 Avios plus $241.70 CAD in taxes and fees. Measured against a one-way cash fare of $3,312 CAD for the same Madrid to Toronto flight, that works out to about 7.58 cents per Avios point.

Madrid to Toronto One-Way Cash Price: $3,312

For this case, I'm comparing a one-way award ticket vs a one-way cash ticket because the cash price isn't hugely inflated but regardless, treat the 7.58 cents as an illustration of the fee savings rather than a precise apples-to-apples value. Either way, the lesson holds. Moving your return off Heathrow and onto a lower-tax departure point is where the real savings sit.

Avios live in a shared family of programs. British Airways, Iberia, Aer Lingus, and Qatar all use the same currency, and balances move between them at a one-to-one rate, so you book each flight through whichever program charges the least. For this Iberia flight, that means booking through Iberia's own program.

Canadians have a few solid ways to build an Avios balance:

  • American Express Membership Rewards points transfer to British Airways Avios at a one-to-one rate.
  • RBC Avion points transfer to Avios at a one-to-one rate.
  • Marriott Bonvoy points transfer to Avios at three-to-one, and Marriott adds 5,000 bonus miles for every 60,000 points you move. That turns a 60,000 point transfer into 25,000 Avios, an effective rate of about 2.4 to one.

Once the Avios land in any of these accounts, you can shift them into Iberia's program to book the Madrid flight.

My Thoughts

I took this Virgin Atlantic flight recently, and it is an easy one to recommend. The business class experience on their Airbus A350 is great and the combination of a reliable nonstop, a true lie-flat seat, and wide-open award space is not something Canadians see all the time on a transatlantic route. Also, the fact that you can get in with Amex points at a one-to-one transfer via the Flying Blue Miles program makes it accessible for a lot of people.

If I were booking it today, I would strongly consider taking the outbound as an award and leaving the return flexible, given how much of the fee burden sits on the Heathrow departure. That is not the only way to play it, but it is where the value is best.

This is also a good reminder of why it pays to belong to more than one airline program. You could book the whole round trip on Virgin Atlantic through Flying Blue and still do very well. But because I also keep a balance of Avios, I have the option to fly home on a different carrier from a lower-tax country, which trims the fees and lifts the value per mile. That flexibility is the real advantage.

The more programs you can move your points into, the more room you have to route around high fees and squeeze the most value out of a points booking, especially on international trips.

The Bottom Line

For 69,500 miles + fees each way, a nonstop lie-flat business class seat between Toronto and London is one of the better transatlantic redemptions available to Canadians right now. Book it through Air France KLM Flying Blue rather than Virgin Atlantic, transfer in from Amex at one-to-one, and you are looking at a comfortable, reliable way to cross the Atlantic without paying full business class cash prices.

As always, do not transfer or buy points until you have a specific flight and date confirmed as available. Points are only worth what you can actually book, so line up the redemption first.

Happy travels!
