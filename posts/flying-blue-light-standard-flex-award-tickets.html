Flying Blue Is Splitting Air France and KLM Award Tickets Into Light, Standard, and Flex Fares

Flying Blue, the loyalty program shared by Air France and KLM, is changing how reward tickets work.

Starting September 8, 2026, every new reward booking on an Air France or KLM operated flight will come in one of three versions: Light, Standard, or Flex. On paper the cheapest option still starts at today's minimum mileage price. In practice, that cheapest option now gives you noticeably less, and getting back what a reward ticket includes today means paying more miles. That is why a lot of travellers are already calling this a devaluation, and once you see it on a real route, it is hard to argue with them.

Here is what is happening, what each fare actually includes, and what it means if you are a Canadian sitting on transferable points.

What Is Actually Changing

From September 8, 2026, Air France and KLM are copying their cash-fare structure over to reward tickets. Instead of one award ticket, you will choose between Light, Standard, and Flex.

A few important details before we get into the fares.

This applies only to new reward bookings made on or after September 8, 2026. Anything you have already booked keeps its original conditions, so there is no need to touch existing trips.

It applies only to flights operated and marketed by Air France or KLM. Reward tickets on partner airlines are not affected and stay exactly as they are today. If your itinerary mixes an Air France or KLM flight with a partner flight, only the Standard fare will be available, and standalone partner flights remain unbranded.

La Première, Air France's top first-class product, is excluded. Every other cabin and booking class is in.

The Three Fares in Plain Terms

Source: Air France

Light is the stripped-down base fare. It is built for someone travelling with just a carry-on in Economy or Premium, or with only one checked bag in Business, who does not expect to change their plans.

Standard is the middle option. It brings back a checked bag and adds the ability to change or refund for a fee.

Flex is the fully flexible fare. Free changes, free refunds, seat selection included, and, notably, a lower mandatory cash contribution when you book. That last point is a real perk, since the cash surcharge on Air France and KLM awards has always been one of the more annoying parts of using Flying Blue miles.

A Real Example: Toronto to Paris in Business

Toronto to Paris in Business Class: Currently 60,000 Miles + fees.

The new fares are not visible on the Air France and KLM sites yet, since they only go live on September 8. But you can see the change clearly by looking at what a Toronto to Paris Business award costs today and lining it up against the new structure.

Right now, a one-way Business Class award from Toronto to Paris prices out at 60,000 miles plus about $556 CAD. That single ticket already includes two checked bags at 32kg each, two hand baggage items, lounge access, SkyPriority, free standard seat selection at check-in, and the ability to change or refund for a €70 fee. In other words, today's 60,000-mile Business award is, feature for feature, the new Business Standard.

From September 8, that same route splits into three:

Each fare still carries the usual surcharges on top of the miles, and Flex is set to come with a lower cash contribution than the others. That is the whole story in one table. The 60,000-mile price tag survives, but only for Light, which strips out most of what made the ticket worth booking.

To keep exactly what you get today, from September 8 onwards you will pay 75,000 miles, a 25% increase for the same seat and the same perks. These figures are for this specific search and will vary by route and date, but the mechanic is the same everywhere the new fares apply.

So Where Is the Devaluation?

Source: Air France

Flying Blue can say it did not raise the number of miles you need for the cheapest seat. This is not the usual devaluation where a flight that costs 60,000 miles today quietly becomes 75,000 tomorrow for the exact same product.

The problem is what those miles now buy. As the Toronto to Paris example shows, the cheapest 60,000-mile fare no longer gets you the ticket you are used to. To match today's experience you move up to Standard and pay 25% more miles, or you jump to Flex and pay nearly double. The number on the cheapest seat did not change, but the seat behind that number got a lot thinner.

The piece that stings most is the non-refundable Light ticket. For a lot of people, the whole appeal of using miles instead of cash was flexibility. An award ticket you could cancel and redeposit was a low-risk way to lock in a plan. A non-refundable Light fare takes that safety net away unless you pay up.

The Canadian Angle

This matters more than usual for Canadians because Flying Blue is one of the easier programs to feed from home. American Express Membership Rewards transfers to Flying Blue, so a lot of Canadian travellers are effectively converting flexible points into Air France and KLM awards for trips to Europe and beyond.

Two things to keep in mind.

First, if you are booking a big transatlantic redemption and there is any chance your plans shift, that non-refundable Light fare is a real risk. Paying into Standard or Flex for the ability to cancel may be worth it on an expensive Business seat.

Second, this only touches Air France and KLM operated flights. If you are routing on a partner such as one of the other SkyTeam carriers, your reward ticket is unchanged, so partner awards may quietly become the more flexible play.

Important Note for Flying Blue Elite Members

If you hold Flying Blue elite status, most of this passes you by. Flying Blue has confirmed that status benefits, such as extra checked baggage and waived change or refund fees where they apply, are unchanged and still kick in on the day of departure. So a Silver member and up will keep a lot of what Light strips out.

This is clearly part of the point: the new structure makes status more valuable by making the base fare thinner.

The Bottom Line

Flying Blue did not raise the sticker price, but it lowered what that price buys, and it removed the flexibility that made award tickets feel safe in the first place. The Toronto to Paris numbers say it plainly: the same Business seat you can book today for 60,000 miles will cost 75,000 after September 8 once you add back the perks that used to be standard.

Whether you call it a repricing or a devaluation, the practical result is the same. Price your next redemption carefully, and lean on your status and on partner awards where it helps.

Happy travels!
