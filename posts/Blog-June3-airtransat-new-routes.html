Air Transat Is Thinking Big: New Routes for Summer 2026 and Winter 2026–27

Air Transat has been on a roll lately, and the announcements just keep coming. Between a summer program that is quietly one of the most ambitious the airline has ever put together and a wave of new winter sun routes, there is a lot to unpack.

Here is a full breakdown of every new route and why some of them are worth getting excited about.

Summer 2026: Where Things Get Really Interesting

Agadir, Morocco (Montreal to Agadir)

Starting June 12, 2026 — once a week

Operated by the narrowbody Airbus A321neo: 2-2 Club Class and 3-3 Economy

Morocco is having a moment globally, and Agadir is a different entry point than the more commonly visited Marrakech or Casablanca.

It is a beach-oriented city with a relaxed atmosphere, warm Atlantic water, and easy access to the Atlas Mountains and the broader country. A weekly nonstop from Montreal makes it a viable standalone destination rather than a layover city.

Air Transat is the only North American carrier flying this route nonstop, so if you want to go direct, this is your only option.

Reykjavík, Iceland (Montreal to Reykjavík)

Starting June 16, 2026 — twice a week

Operated by the narrowbody Airbus A321: 3-3 Economy only — no Club Class available on this route

Iceland needs little introduction at this point, but a nonstop from Montreal twice a week during summer is still worth flagging.

June and July mean near-endless daylight, prime conditions for the Golden Circle, and the chance to experience the midnight sun. Iceland is one of those places that rewards going directly. The country is small enough that landing in Reykjavík and being somewhere stunning within an hour is entirely realistic.

Air Canada also flies this route nonstop from Montreal, so it is worth comparing schedules and fares between the two.

Dakar, Senegal (Montreal to Dakar)

Starting June 17, 2026 — twice a week

Operated by the narrowbody Airbus A321neo: 2-2 Club Class and 3-3 Economy

Senegal remains largely off the radar for most Canadian travellers, and that is part of the appeal. Dakar is a vibrant, culturally rich city with incredible music, food, and art scenes, and it serves as a natural gateway to the wider region.

Two weekly nonstops from Montreal make it far more accessible than it has ever been from Canada. Air Transat is the only Canadian airline flying nonstop to sub-Saharan Africa, making this route one of a kind.

Tirana, Albania (Toronto to Tirana)

Starting June 18, 2026 — once a week

Operated by the widebody Airbus A330: 2-2-2 Club Class and 3-3-3 Economy

This is the headline of the summer program, and it deserves to be. Air Transat becomes the first airline to operate a nonstop flight between North America and Albania, a historic route for Canadian travellers.

Albania has been quietly building a reputation as one of Europe's best-value destinations: dramatic coastlines on the Adriatic and Ionian seas, UNESCO-listed cities like Berat and Gjirokastrër, Mediterranean food, and easy access to neighbouring Greece and Montenegro.

Until now, getting there from Canada required at least one connection. That changes this June. No other airline flies this route nonstop from anywhere in North America.

Winter 2026–2027: Sun Season Gets a Major Upgrade

Los Cabos (Montreal to Los Cabos)

Starting December 10, 2026 — once a week

Operated by the narrowbody Airbus A321neo: 2-2 Club Class and 3-3 Economy

Los Cabos has steadily climbed the ranks of Mexico's most popular destinations, and for good reason. It offers resort luxury on one end and rugged desert-meets-ocean scenery on the other.

A nonstop from Montreal is long overdue. Air Canada is also launching a nonstop Montreal to Los Cabos flight for winter 2026-27, so there will be options to compare when planning your trip.

Aruba (Montreal to Oranjestad)

Starting December 12, 2026 — once a week

Operated by the narrowbody Airbus A321neo: 2-2 Club Class and 3-3 Economy

Aruba sits outside the main hurricane belt, which makes it one of the most reliable winter sun destinations in the Caribbean. Consistent dry weather, calm turquoise water, and a relaxed pace make this one of the smarter picks for a February or March escape.

A nonstop from Montreal removes the last excuse not to go. Air Canada is also adding a nonstop Montreal to Aruba flight this winter, meaning more seats and more scheduling flexibility for travellers flying out of YUL.

Las Palmas, Canary Islands

Montreal to Las Palmas: starting December 12, 2026 — once a week

Toronto to Las Palmas: starting December 13, 2026 — once a week

Operated by the narrowbody Airbus A321neo: 2-2 Club Class and 3-3 Economy

This one might be the most underrated announcement in the whole bunch. The Canary Islands sit off the northwest coast of Africa, giving them a mild, spring-like climate year-round, even in January.

Las Palmas, on Gran Canaria, is a proper city with beaches, a beautiful old quarter, and easy access to the rest of the archipelago. For Canadians looking for something different from the usual Caribbean or Mexico formula, this one is worth serious consideration.

No other Canadian airline is flying to Las Palmas nonstop.

Barbados (Montreal to Bridgetown)

Starting December 13, 2026 — twice a week

Operated by the narrowbody Airbus A321neo: 2-2 Club Class and 3-3 Economy

Barbados has a distinct character that sets it apart from most Caribbean islands, with a mix of British colonial history, world-class rum, incredible food, and some of the best beaches in the region.

The twice-weekly frequency from Montreal makes it accessible for a one-week trip without awkward connection logistics. This will be the first direct winter scheduled service from Montreal to Barbados, and Air Transat is the only carrier flying it nonstop from YUL.

The Bottom Line

What stands out about these upcoming Air Transat routes is the variety. The airline is opening up West Africa, the Canary Islands, the Balkans, and North Africa. For travellers who have been building itineraries around connections and layovers, these nonstops represent a real shift in what is possible.

The summer routes are either live or just around the corner. The winter routes are already bookable. If any of these destinations have been on your list, now is a good time to start watching fares.

Happy travels!
