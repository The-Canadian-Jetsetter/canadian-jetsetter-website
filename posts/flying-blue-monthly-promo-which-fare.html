Which Fare Do You Get When You Book a Flying Blue Monthly Promo Reward?

Since September 8, 2026, Air France and KLM award tickets no longer come as a single ticket. Every new reward booking on an Air France or KLM operated flight now splits into three versions: Light, Standard, and Flex.

I broke that change down in a recent post, but it raises an obvious question for anyone who books the monthly Flying Blue Promo Rewards: which of these three fares do you actually end up with when you redeem on a promo? Read on to find out, because there is also a small twist that works in your favour.

Read on to learn more, or visit Air France's website below to search for redemptions!

A Quick Refresher on Light, Standard, and Flex

Light is the stripped-down base fare, and what it strips out depends on your cabin. In Economy and Premium it leaves you with a carry-on only and no checked bag, while in Business you keep one checked bag of up to 32kg. Across every cabin the Light fare is unchangeable and non-refundable, so the flexibility that made award tickets feel low-risk is the first thing to go. Business Light does hold on to SkyPriority and its single checked bag, but you lose lounge access and the ability to change or cancel, which is a lot to give up on an expensive long-haul seat.

Standard is the middle option and the one most people picture when they think of a normal award ticket. It brings back a checked bag, adds SkyPriority in the cabins where that applies, and lets you change or refund for a €70 fee, which works out to roughly $105 CAD. Flex sits at the top with free changes, free refunds, seat selection included, and a lower mandatory cash contribution when you book. The catch with the whole restructure is that the cheapest sticker price now buys you less than a reward ticket did before September 8, which is why a lot of people are calling it a devaluation.

So Which Fare Does the Promo Book Into?

Good news: when you book one of the monthly Promo Rewards, you land on the Standard fare, not the bare-bones Light fare.

You keep the checked bag, SkyPriority, seat selection, and the ability to change or refund for that €70 fee, which is exactly the ticket most people would have wanted anyway. And as you will see next, the discount can leave you paying fewer miles for that Standard fare than the Light fare would cost on the very same flight.

The Funny Part: Light Can Cost You More Miles

Here is where it gets interesting. Because the promo discount is applied to the Standard fare, the promo Standard can actually come in cheaper than the plain Light fare on the very same flight. Take this month's Vancouver Premium Economy promo. When I pulled up a Paris to Vancouver Premium Economy award, the Light fare priced out at 40,000 miles plus $461.90 CAD, the promo Standard fare came in at 30,000 miles plus $461.90 CAD, and the Flex fare sat at 85,000 miles plus $197.10 CAD.

That is worth sitting with for a second. The Light fare, which gives you no checked bag, no advance seat selection, and no ability to change or cancel, costs 40,000 miles. The promo Standard fare, which includes two checked bags, SkyPriority, seat selection, and a changeable and refundable ticket, costs 30,000 miles. You would be paying 10,000 more miles to end up on the worse ticket, so there is really no decision to agonize over. On this promo the discount lands you on the better fare for fewer miles.

A Display Quirk to Watch For

One thing tripped me up while I was searching.

When you pull up these promos on the Air France website, the results page usually shows the base price for the itinerary rather than the promo price. In my Paris to Vancouver search the summary listed 40,000 miles plus taxes and fees for a one-way Premium Economy flight, and it was only after I clicked into the itinerary that the promo fare appeared at 30,000 miles plus taxes and fees.

So if you see the full price on the search results page and assume the promo has vanished, click into the itinerary before you give up, because the discounted fare shows up on the next screen.

This Month's September Promo

For September, three Canadian cities are featured with discounted Promo Rewards to Europe:

  • Montreal to Europe: 18,750 miles + fees in Economy
  • Toronto to Europe: 30,000 miles + fees in Premium Economy (Flying Blue Extra members only)
  • Vancouver to Europe: 30,000 miles + fees in Premium Economy

These must be booked by September 30, 2026, for travel through February 28, 2027, subject to availability. Montreal and Vancouver are open to any Flying Blue member, while Toronto is reserved for Flying Blue Extra subscribers.

My Thoughts

It is not often that a program change quietly works in your favour, but this is one of those cases. The new fare structure is a step down overall, and I stand by calling it a devaluation, especially the non-refundable Light fare that takes away the safety net a lot of people valued.

For the monthly promos specifically, though, you are getting the Standard fare, which is the one most people would have chosen anyway, and on the routes I checked you are paying fewer miles for it than the Light fare would cost.

If you collect American Express Membership Rewards, remember that those points transfer to Flying Blue at 1:1, so topping up to reach one of these promos is straightforward.

The Bottom Line

Book a monthly Flying Blue Promo Reward and you land on the Standard fare rather than Light, which means a checked bag, SkyPriority, and a changeable and refundable ticket.

Just watch the search results page, which shows the base price until you click into the itinerary and the promo fare appears. Award pricing and availability shift by route and date, so confirm the numbers on the Air France website before you book.

Happy travels!
