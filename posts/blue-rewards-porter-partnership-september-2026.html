Blue Rewards Names Porter Its Preferred Airline Partner: What Canadians Need to Know

Blue Rewards and Porter Airlines have announced a partnership that names Porter the preferred airline partner of the program.

As of September 24, 2026, members who link their Blue Rewards and VIPorter accounts can earn VIPorter points faster and pick up bonus Blue Points when they redeem for Porter flights.

I have never covered Blue Rewards on the blog before, so I want to start with what the program actually is, since it is the recent rebrand of Air Miles, and then break down exactly what this Porter tie-up puts in your pocket.

Header Image Source: Blue Rewards.

What is the Blue Rewards Program?

Source: Blue Rewards.

If the name is new to you, that is because it is new. Blue Rewards is the rebrand of Air Miles, and it launched in June 2026. It continues to be run by a subsidiary of BMO, which acquired Air Miles back in 2023.

The most important change for former collectors is the currency.

The old split between Cash Miles and Dream Miles is gone, and everything now sits in a single Blue Points balance. Existing balances carried over with no loss in value: since June 1, 2026, Air Miles Cash and Dream miles have converted at a rate of 95 Air Miles for every 1,500 Blue Points. The partner network came along too, with more than 400 brands from the Air Miles era still on board, though one familiar name is now missing. Shell Canada exited in 2026, and its fuel rewards have shifted to Scene+.

How Do You Earn & Redeem Blue Rewards?

Source: Blue Rewards.

You earn Blue Points in a few different ways. The most common are paying with a linked card at participating partner brands, scanning grocery and everyday receipts through the Blue Rewards app, and shopping online through Blue Rewards Shops. You do not need to bank with BMO to join either, since the program is open to any Canadian resident.

On value, Blue Points sit at the everyday end of the loyalty world rather than the premium travel end. The baseline redemption rate is 1,500 Blue Points for $10 CAD when redeemed in-store or on eGift cards, which lands at about two-thirds of a cent per point, or roughly 0.67 cents.

That is the same redemption value as BMO Rewards, the separate currency tied to cards like the BMO Ascend and BMO eclipse. The two programs are worth the same when you cash out, but they are not interchangeable, so you cannot move a balance from one to the other. Other redemption types, including travel, run on separate rates. For context, Aeroplan points are generally valued at around 2 cents each, so Blue Points are a more modest currency built around regular spending.

Travel booking now runs through an Expedia-powered platform called Blue Rewards Travel, and it comes with a real improvement over the old program. You can now cover 100 percent of a flight with points, taxes and fees included, where Air Miles required you to pay taxes in cash. That is a welcome change for anyone redeeming for travel. Porter came on board as a partner at launch, and this week's news elevates it to the preferred airline partner slot.

Extra Earning With BMO

Source: Blue Rewards.

Where BMO customers pull ahead is on the earning side. The new BMO Blue Rewards credit cards carry a 10x accelerator at participating partners. The Blue World Elite card earns 10 Blue Points per dollar on up to $25,000 CAD in combined spending per year, and the no-fee Blue card earns 5 Blue Points per dollar on up to $15,000 CAD per year.

Beyond the credit cards, a BMO Blue Rewards Chequing Account earns points on everyday banking as well, including eligible debit purchases, bill payments, and direct deposits. If you already bank with BMO, this is the easiest way to add Blue Points to your balance without changing how you spend.

What the Porter Partnership Adds

Source: Porter Airlines.

The whole thing hinges on linking your Blue Rewards and VIPorter accounts. Once they are linked, four benefits open up.

A welcome bonus for linking. Members can earn 500 Blue Points and 500 VIPorter points simply for connecting the two accounts and earning at least 1 Blue Point at any partner. It is a small amount, but it is effectively free for anyone who holds both.

Faster VIPorter earning on Porter flights. The base earn on Porter flights is 5 to 7 VIPorter points per dollar spent, excluding taxes and fees. Linked members earn on top of that:

  • Link and pay with an eligible linked BMO payment card, and you earn 1 additional VIPorter point per dollar.
  • Link without that card, and you earn 1 additional VIPorter point per $2.

On the base earning tier, the top rate lands you roughly 20 percent more points per flight.

Bonus Blue Points when you redeem for Porter flights. This is the most interesting piece. When you redeem Blue Points for eligible Porter flights through Blue Rewards Travel, you earn bonus Blue Points scaled to the value of the booking.

Porter flight value Bonus Blue Points Approx. value back
Up to $350 CAD 1,000 ~$7 CAD
$351 – $450 CAD 3,750 ~$25 CAD
$451 CAD or more 7,500 ~$50 CAD

Limited-time offer: Until November 30, 2026, Blue Rewards members with an eligible BMO payment card can earn up to 1,500 additional bonus Blue Points, worth about $10 CAD, on an eligible Porter redemption made through Blue Rewards Travel.

Blue Points to VIPorter conversion is coming. In the coming months, linked members will be able to convert Blue Points into VIPorter points at a 2:1 ratio, meaning 2 Blue Points will become 1 VIPorter point. No firm launch date has been shared yet.

One thing to underline: if you carry a BMO Blue Rewards card, you get the strongest version of nearly every perk here. It is the difference between earning 1 extra VIPorter point per dollar instead of per $2 on Porter flights, and it is the key that unlocks the limited-time redemption top-up. If you are weighing whether to link your accounts, holding a BMO card tilts the math in your favour.

So Is It Worth Chasing?

For the points-optimizing crowd that reads this blog, keep your expectations calibrated. The earning bump on Porter flights is real but small, and the redemption bonuses, while nice, are paid out in Blue Points at that modest baseline value. The coming 2:1 conversion is closer to break-even than to a windfall once you account for VIPorter's dynamic pricing, so I would not treat it as a reason to hoard Blue Points.

Where this does matter is for the very large group of former Air Miles collectors who now hold Blue Points and already fly Porter. For that person, linking the accounts, taking the welcome bonus, and grabbing the redemption bonuses on a flight they were going to book anyway is a straightforward win at zero extra effort. If they also carry a BMO Blue Rewards card, the return climbs further.

My Thoughts

Porter keeps stacking up partnerships, and naming itself the preferred airline of a program with millions of members is a smart move for filling seats on its growing network. It also makes sense given who is behind both programs. BMO owns Blue Rewards and already issues Porter's co-branded credit card, the BMO VIPorter World Elite Card, so tying the two loyalty programs together is a logical next step rather than a surprise.

For readers who live in the Aeroplan and Flying Blue world, this is not going to change how you play the game. For the enormous former Air Miles base, though, it is the clearest reason yet to actually use those Blue Points on travel rather than letting them drift toward gift cards.

The redemption bonuses are the standout, the earning bump is minor, and the conversion feature is worth a second look once the details are locked in.

The Bottom Line

If you hold Blue Points and you fly Porter, link the two accounts, claim the welcome bonus, and put the redemption bonuses to work. If you carry an eligible BMO card, you get the enhanced earning tier and the limited-time top-up, so use it before it ends on November 30, 2026.

Just keep in mind that Blue Points are a modest currency, so redeem them with a specific trip in mind rather than chasing bonuses for their own sake.

Happy travels!
