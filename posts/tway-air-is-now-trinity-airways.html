T'way Air Is Now Trinity Airways: What the Rebrand Means for Canadians Flying to Seoul

South Korea has a new airline name to learn, and it is one that already reaches Canada.

As of September 10, 2026, the carrier that used to be called T'way Air now flies as Trinity Airways. The airline serves Vancouver, which remains its only destination in North America, so this rebrand is worth understanding if Seoul is anywhere on your travel list.

This is more than a fresh coat of paint. Trinity Airways is repositioning itself away from the low-cost label toward what it calls a "selective service carrier," and there are some interesting cabin changes on the horizon, especially in business class. Here is what I know so far, and what it could mean for the flight to Seoul.

Image Header Source: Trinity Airways

Where Trinity Airways Came From

Source: Skytrax.

The airline may be new in name, but it has been flying for well over a decade.

It started as Hansung Airlines before relaunching as T'way Air in 2010, where the "t" stood for together, today, and tomorrow. It began with two Boeing 737-800 aircraft on a single Gimpo to Jeju route, added its first international flight to Bangkok in 2011, and by 2019 had grown into the third largest of South Korea's low-cost carriers in the international market. The long-haul chapter opened in 2022, when the airline took its first Airbus A330 and announced a Seoul to Sydney route.

Its most notable long-haul addition came in 2024, when it launched Seoul to Frankfurt. That route was handed to the airline as part of a concession tied to the merger of Korean Air and Asiana Airlines, a deal set to be finalized on December 17, 2026, since both giants served Frankfurt and regulators wanted a competitor on the route. Ownership then changed hands too. T'way Air was bought by the Daemyung Sono Group, a South Korean hospitality company that later renamed itself the Sono Trinity Group, and that hotel and resort connection is a big part of why Trinity Airways now talks about pairing flights with accommodation.

The Rebrand and Your Booking

Source: Trinity Airways.

The change became official on September 10, 2026, when the first flights operated under the new Trinity Airways name and livery. For anyone with an existing booking, the practical details stay the same.

The airline keeps its TW flight code, its flight numbers, and its schedules, so tickets purchased under the old name remain valid. If you booked before the switch, your confirmation may still show T'way Air, and that is nothing to worry about.

What a "Selective Service Carrier" Is Supposed to Mean

Source: Trinity Airways.

Trinity Airways is trying to carve out a spot between the full-service carriers and the budget airlines. Its idea of a "selective service carrier" is to keep fares competitive and the product simple on short-haul routes, while adding more comfort and service on the long-haul flights where passengers tend to value it most.

In practice, that includes premium check-in at Incheon, an expanded onboard meal program on longer routes, plans for WiFi, and a lounge at the airport down the road. A points program has also been mentioned as a future addition. It is a promising list, though most of it is still rolling out, so I would treat it as a direction of travel rather than a finished product for now.

The New A330neo Business Class Is the Part I Am Watching

Source: Trinity Airways.

Here is where it gets interesting. Alongside the rebrand, Trinity Airways confirmed it is bringing in new Airbus A330-900neo aircraft with its own custom cabins, and the business class choice is unusual.

Trinity Airways is the launch customer for a seat called the Optimares SoFab. The headline is that it lies fully flat, which is a real upgrade over a standard recliner. The catch is that it does not recline in the normal sense at all. There is no range of positions in between. You are either sitting upright or the seat is folded down into a flat bed, and that is it. On top of that, these new planes will not have seatback entertainment screens.

To soften that last point, the airline has said it plans to introduce WiFi starting with these A330neo aircraft, which the current fleet does not offer. So the trade is a lie-flat bed and connectivity in exchange for a very pared-back seat and no built-in screen. This reminds me of ZIPAIR Tokyo's "ZIP Full-Flat" product, which is not a traditional business class but rather a lie-flat seat with no seatback entertainment, though onboard WiFi is included. Trinity Airways seems to be keeping its business class a little more full-fledged than that, but seeing these updates is interesting, and I am curious to see how it feels in person.

One thing to keep in mind: these new interiors are aimed at the airline's longer routes, and Vancouver sits on that long-haul list alongside cities like Rome, Paris, and Barcelona. Whether and when Vancouver specifically moves to the A330-900neo has not been confirmed, so I would not book a 2026 trip expecting the new bed just yet.

What Vancouver Flyers Get Right Now

For the moment, the Vancouver route runs four times a week on the older Airbus A330-300. It is a two-cabin aircraft with 347 seats. The business cabin is compact, with 12 seats in a 2-2-2 layout, a generous 59 inch pitch, and a recline of 165 degrees. I want to be clear that this is a deep recliner and not a true lie-flat bed, so it sits a step below the fully flat business seats you will find on Air Canada or Korean Air. That said, at the fares Trinity Airways tends to charge, it can still be strong value for a crossing over the Pacific.

T'way Air's A330-300 Business Class. Source: Airways Magazine.

Economy holds 335 seats in a 2-4-2 layout with a 32 inch pitch, which is a touch roomier than the long-haul standard. As with any budget-leaning carrier, expect the base fare to cover the seat while extras such as bags, meals, and seat selection are added on.

And the fares are where Trinity Airways makes its case. Take an upcoming nonstop round trip from Vancouver to Seoul in economy:

Source: Google Flights.

Trinity Airways came in at $947 CAD round trip. The next cheapest nonstop option was Air Canada at $1,152 CAD, which means Trinity undercut it by roughly $205 CAD.

The gap widens in business class:

Source: Google Flights.

Here Trinity Airways sat at $3,622 CAD round trip, while the next cheapest nonstop itinerary from Korean Air came in at $5,413 CAD. That is roughly $1,791 CAD less for the same nonstop routing, though remember that at this price you are still trading a true flat bed for a recliner.

My Thoughts

I find what Trinity Airways is trying to do here interesting.

Moving up from a pure low-cost model gives Canadians another nonstop option to Seoul, and whether the "selective service carrier" idea actually lands will come down to how much of that promised list arrives and how the fares hold up. For now, the value is clearly still there, and the economy price in those screenshots is hard to argue with for a direct flight.

The business class story is the part I am most curious about. On the current A330-300, you are getting a comfortable recliner rather than a flat bed, so I would weigh that $3,622 CAD fare against what a true lie-flat seat on a competitor costs before deciding. The upcoming Optimares seat on the A330neo is the more intriguing prospect, since it delivers an actual flat bed, but the lack of recline, the absence of a seatback screen, and the reliance on WiFi that is not yet in service leave me with questions. I will hold my judgment until I can try it.

For most travelers, the takeaway is simple. If you want the lowest reasonable fare to Seoul on a nonstop flight, Trinity Airways deserves a look. If a fully flat business class is your priority, keep an eye on the A330neo rollout, but do not assume it is coming to Vancouver on any particular date.

The Bottom Line

T'way Air becoming Trinity Airways is more than a name change. It signals a real push upmarket, with premium touches, a new meal program, WiFi, and eventually a flat-bed business class on new A330-900neo aircraft.

For Canadians, the immediate story is unchanged value on nonstop Vancouver to Seoul flights, with economy fares that remain very competitive. The more interesting chapter, that unusual new business class, is still on its way, and I will be watching to see if and when it reaches this side of the Pacific.

Happy travels!
