Buying Extra Legroom on Canadian Airlines: Air Canada, WestJet, Porter, and Flair Compared

If you have squeezed into an economy seat lately, you already know the truth: they are not getting any bigger. Legroom has been quietly shrinking for years, and every major Canadian carrier now sells a way to buy some of it back. The trouble is that each airline calls its version something different, prices it differently, and delivers a different amount of actual space.

So I pulled the numbers on four of the carriers most of us fly out of here in Canada: Air Canada, WestJet, Porter, and Flair. Here is what you are actually paying for, and where the extra legroom is worth it.

A quick note on exit rows

Before we dig in, one thing to flag. On most of these airlines the exit row comes with a lot more pitch than the paid legroom seats, sometimes reaching around 38 to 39" depending on the carrier and aircraft. That is a bit of a special case, so in this guide I am focusing on the more normal extra legroom seats you buy as an upgrade, not the exit row.

Header Image Source: WestJet.

Air Canada: Preferred Seats

Source: Air Canada.

Air Canada's legroom product is called Preferred Seats. You can buy one on just about any Air Canada flight, subject to availability, and they sit in the exit rows, in select bulkhead rows, and in the first few rows of the cabin.

The pitch here is up to 10 cm (4") of extra space between seats, plus earlier access to overhead bin space through Zone 3 boarding and a quicker exit on arrival. Prices run from $20 CAD to $199 CAD depending on the route and seat.

A few things worth knowing. Preferred Seats are complimentary for Aeroplan Elite Members and anyone travelling on a Latitude or comfort fare on select routes. They are not available on flights operated by Star Alliance or other airline partners, so if your Air Canada ticket is flown by a partner carrier, this option disappears. And you cannot buy a Preferred Seat with Aeroplan points, though you do still earn points on the flight itself.

WestJet: Extended Comfort

Source: WestJet.

WestJet is where the naming gets confusing, so let me clear it up first. WestJet sells both Extended Comfort and Preferred Economy seats, but only one of them is an actual legroom upgrade. Preferred Economy simply puts you closer to the front of the cabin for a faster exit, with standard legroom. Extended Comfort is the one you want if space is the goal.

Extended Comfort gives you an extra 3 to 6" of legroom depending on the aircraft, along with Zone 2 priority boarding, early overhead bin access, priority security screening at select airports, a quicker exit, and a complimentary alcoholic beverage during inflight service. On the Boeing 787, that works out to a seat pitch of 89 cm (35") against 79 cm (31") in standard economy, with recline of up to 15 cm (6"). On the 737 MAX 8, Extended Comfort pitch ranges from 86 to 91 cm (34 to 36") versus 76 cm (30") in standard economy.

The pricing is consistent, at least: an Extended Comfort seat costs $20 CAD to $400 CAD regardless of whether you booked UltraBasic, Econo, or EconoFlex. That is also the most expensive range of any carrier here, so you are paying for the space and those extra perks.

When you book on WestJet's website, Extended Comfort actually shows up as its own fare tier sitting between Economy and Premium. You do not have to book it that way, though. If you book any Economy fare (UltraBasic, Econo, or EconoFlex), you can still go into the seat map and select an Extended Comfort seat directly. Exit row seats are the other legroom option and run $5 CAD to $400 CAD. Worth noting that if you skip seat selection entirely, WestJet assigns you a seat at no charge.

Porter: PorterClassic Stretch

Source: Porter Airlines.

Porter's extra legroom seat is called PorterClassic Stretch, and is only available on the Embraer E195-E2. Porter tends to fly that aircraft on its longer routes, so you are more likely to find Stretch on a longer flight, though I would confirm the aircraft type on your specific route rather than assume it.

When you can get it, the upgrade is a clean one. Stretch seats offer 34" of pitch against 30" in a standard PorterClassic seat, with recline of up to 4" versus up to 2". There are only 20 of these seats on the aircraft, located in rows 5 to 7 and in exit rows 13 and 14, so they go quickly. Seat width is identical at 46.5 cm (18.3"), so this is purely a legroom and recline play.

On price, a Stretch seat runs $30 CAD to $100 CAD on Basic, Standard, and Flexible fares, and it is included at no extra cost if you are travelling on a Freedom fare.

Flair: Preferred Seats

Source: Flair Airlines.

Flair keeps its standard pitch tight, in the range of 29 to 30", which is about what you would expect from an ultra low cost carrier. The upside is that Flair gives you the most granular menu of upgrade options, so you can pay for exactly the amount of space you want.

There are a few Preferred Seat tiers to pick from. The Extra Legroom seats offer up to 33" for $25 CAD to $59 CAD. Front Row seats also reach up to 33" and cost $50 CAD to $94 CAD. And Front Cabin seats range from 29 to 33" at $20 CAD to $59 CAD, which is closer to a front-of-plane convenience play than a true legroom bump. Standard seat selection, for reference, runs $15 CAD to $44 CAD.

If you want more room on Flair without going for the exit row, the Extra Legroom tier is the best value of the bunch.

How They Stack Up

Here is the quick side by side. All prices are one-way and subject to change, so treat them as a snapshot rather than a fixed rate.

My Thoughts

Whether one of these seats is worth buying really comes down to two things: how tall you are and how long you are flying. If you are on the shorter side and your flight is only around three hours, I would not bother paying extra for any of them. You will be off the plane before the tight legroom has much chance to bug you, and that money is better kept in your pocket.

The calculation flips once you are taller or facing a long-haul flight, whether that is a long trip across North America or a longer haul abroad. On a travel day like that, paying an extra few hundred dollars for meaningfully more room and a more comfortable ride can be money well spent.

The other thing I would flag is naming. WestJet's Preferred Economy and Air Canada's Preferred Seats sound similar but are not the same product, and WestJet's Preferred Economy is not even a legroom upgrade. Read the fine print before you pay, because “preferred” does not always mean “more room.”

The Bottom Line

Every Canadian carrier now sells legroom back to you, but the value varies a lot. Flair has the most tiers to choose from and the lowest prices overall, WestJet's Extended Comfort wins on the full package, Porter's Stretch is the best deal when you can get it, and Air Canada's Preferred Seat is free for Aeroplan elites.

My advice is simple: figure out whether you are paying for legroom, a faster exit, or the perks around it, because those are three different things dressed up in similar names.

Happy travels!
