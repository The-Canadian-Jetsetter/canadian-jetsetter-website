WestJet Now Offers Cancel For Any Reason

WestJet rolled out a new feature last week: Cancel For Any Reason (CFAR), available now on WestJet.com and the WestJet app. It's powered by HTS (Hopper Technology Solutions), and it does what it says.

Cancel your flight for any reason up to 24 hours before departure and get a percentage of your money back to your original payment method.

Here's what you need to know.

How It Works

Source: WestJet

CFAR is available on Econo and EconoFlex fares. At checkout, you choose between an 80% or 100% refund tier and pay a fee. If you need to cancel, you do it through the link in your WestJet confirmation email or through Manage Trips on WestJet.com. The refund then processes automatically.

The refund covers your base fare, applicable taxes, and any seat selection fees added at the time of booking. The CFAR fee itself is non-refundable.

A few other details worth knowing: CFAR must be purchased at the time of booking and cannot be added afterward. It applies to all passengers on the booking, and exercising it cancels the entire itinerary. You cannot cancel just one leg. The full booking must be paid by credit card if you opt in.

What It Costs: Real Examples by Route Type

The fee varies depending on your route, travel dates, fare, and number of passengers. We checked a few routes to give you a sense of the range. All examples below are for Econo fares, round trip, one guest.

One thing worth flagging: not every booking shows both tiers. Some routes only offered the 80% option when we checked. It doesn't appear to be something you can control. It just depends on what WestJet and HTS make available for that particular itinerary at the time you're searching it.

As expected, the fee scales with distance and ticket value. On a domestic route, you're looking at under $100 for full coverage. On a transatlantic or transpacific booking where your base fare could easily run $1,000 or more per person, the math gets more interesting.

A Few Fine Print Items

If you change your itinerary after booking, CFAR still applies, but your cancellation window may shorten. Your refund will also be based on the original ticket price, not the modified fare. If you paid extra for a fare change, that portion may be refundable separately through WestJet.

CFAR does not apply if WestJet cancels your flight, or if the airline makes a change that already entitles you to compensation under their policies or Canadian passenger rights rules. In those cases, your regular passenger rights apply regardless of whether you purchased CFAR.

Do Other Canadian Airlines Offer This?

WestJet isn't the only Canadian carrier working with HTS on flexibility products, though the offerings vary by airline.

Air Canada has offered Cancel For Any Reason through HTS since 2023, making it one of the earlier adopters in Canada. If you're booking on Air Canada, you'll find a similar CFAR option at checkout, also powered by HTS.

On a sample Montreal to Toulouse round trip in Economy Standard, the 100% refundable tier came in at $154 per passenger and the 80% tier at $95.

Air Canada's CFAR option at checkout, powered by HTS.

Porter launched their HTS products in early May 2026, just a few weeks before WestJet's CFAR rollout. Porter actually offers three different flexibility options at checkout, which is worth understanding before you book.

The first is Delay and Cancellation Assistance, powered by HTS. This one kicks in when your flight is cancelled or delayed by more than two hours on the day of travel. HTS monitors your flight, notifies you if there's a disruption, and arranges a rebooking on any airline at no additional cost up to a specified maximum per passenger.

On a sample Toronto to Calgary booking it came in at $63 per passenger for the entire booking. If you're not satisfied with the rebooking options, you can take a cash refund instead. Porter notes the product is separate from your rights under Canada's Air Passenger Protection Regulations and may cover disruptions that APPR doesn't.

Porter's Delay and Cancellation Assistance at checkout, powered by HTS.

The second is a Flight Change Option, which gives you unlimited changes up to 24 hours before departure with no change fee (fare difference still applies), or the ability to cancel for a full flight credit. This one starts from $45.20 per passenger per direction on the same sample booking.

The third is a Flight Change + Refund Option at $146.90 per passenger for the entire booking for this example itinerary. This one combines unlimited changes with a full cash refund if you cancel for any reason, with no claims or forms required. In practice, this functions similarly to CFAR on WestJet or Air Canada.

So if you're flying Porter and want the closest equivalent to what WestJet and Air Canada offer through HTS, the Flight Change + Refund Option is likely the one to look at.

Flair has its own flexibility product called TravelFlex, which lets you change your flight once with no change fee, though you still pay any fare difference. That one appears to be Flair's own product rather than an HTS offering.

Flair does also offer the HTS Disruption Guarantee at checkout, which works similarly to Porter's Delay and Cancellation Assistance: if your flight is cancelled or delayed by two or more hours on the day of travel, HTS will rebook you on any airline up to a set cost per passenger, or offer a cash refund of a percentage of your original ticket if you prefer.

Flair's Disruption Guarantee at checkout, powered by HTS.

The short version: flexibility products at checkout have become fairly common across Canadian carriers. The specifics vary quite a bit by airline, so it's worth taking a look at what's on offer when you book, regardless of which airline you're flying.

Worth Adding?

That depends on your trip and your risk tolerance. For a flexible, low-stakes booking you probably wouldn't think twice. For a big international trip with a lot of moving parts, or one you're booking well in advance, the math is worth doing before you click past it at checkout.

Happy travels!
