Air Canada's Summer 2027 Expansion: New Non-Stops to China, Norway, Ireland, Switzerland, and Croatia

Air Canada revealed its Summer 2027 international schedule today, and the airline is calling it the most extensive intercontinental expansion in its history.

Air Canada is adding five new international destinations and seven new routes across its three hubs in Vancouver, Toronto, and Montréal, along with a handful of schedule boosts on routes it already flies.

For us Canadians, the headline here is direct connections. Several of these destinations have never had non-stop service from Canada before, which removes a connection through Europe or the United States and as well, these open up fresh Aeroplan redemption options on Air Canada metal. It is worth noting up front that most of these are seasonal summer routes, and two of them (Guangzhou and Oslo) are still subject to government approval.

Here is everything that was announced, hub by hub, with the schedule for each new route.

Header Image Source: Air Canada.

The Five New Destinations

Here are the five brand-new destinations at a glance:

  • Vancouver to Guangzhou, China (year-round, begins May 4, 2027)
  • Toronto to Oslo, Norway (seasonal, June to October 2027)
  • Toronto to Shannon, Ireland (seasonal, June to October 2027)
  • Montréal to Basel, Switzerland (seasonal, June to October 2027)
  • Montréal to Dubrovnik, Croatia (seasonal, May to October 2027)

On top of these, Air Canada is launching a new Toronto to Nice route and boosting several existing services, which I have broken out further down.

Vancouver to Guangzhou, China

Guangzhou becomes Air Canada's third destination in mainland China, joining Beijing and Shanghai, and the airline says it will be the only carrier in North America flying there non-stop. This is the one year-round addition in the bunch, operating on the Boeing 787 Dreamliner with Signature (business), Premium Economy, and Economy cabins. Guangzhou sits in the Pearl River Delta, a major manufacturing and technology region, so expect a mix of business and leisure demand. This route is still subject to government approval.

Toronto to Oslo, Norway

Oslo gives Air Canada its first non-stop flights to Norway and, by the airline's own account, completes its service to all three Scandinavian capitals alongside Stockholm and Copenhagen. This one is interesting for a different reason: it is operated on the new Airbus A321XLR, a narrowbody flying a transatlantic route, with two cabins including a lie-flat Signature business seat. It runs seasonally from early June to mid-October. Like Guangzhou, the Oslo route is subject to government approval.

Toronto to Shannon, Ireland

Shannon is Air Canada's second Irish destination after Dublin, and it serves as a gateway to Ireland's west coast, the Cliffs of Moher, and the Wild Atlantic Way. It is another A321XLR route with two cabins and a lie-flat Signature seat, running seasonally from mid-June to early October.

Toronto to Nice, France

This is a new route rather than a new destination, since Air Canada already flies Montréal to Nice. Adding the Toronto service means the airline will offer a combined daily Canada to Nice service through the summer. It is operated on the wide-body Airbus A330-300 with Signature, Premium Economy, and Economy cabins, running from mid-June to late October.

Montréal to Basel, Switzerland

Basel rounds out Air Canada's Swiss network, giving it all three major cities alongside Zurich and Geneva. The city sits on the Rhine at the point where Switzerland, France, and Germany meet, so it is a convenient jumping-off point for a wide chunk of central Europe. This is another A321XLR route with a lie-flat Signature cabin, running mid-June to late October.

Montréal to Dubrovnik, Croatia

Dubrovnik, known as the Pearl of the Adriatic, is the destination most clearly aimed at leisure travellers. It is served by the wide-body Boeing 787 Dreamliner with three cabins, and it has the longest season of the new routes, running from mid-May all the way to late October.

Other Schedule Changes

Source: Air Canada.

Beyond the headline routes, Air Canada is making a number of adjustments to services it already operates, drawing on both its formal announcement and a follow-up note to customers:

  • Toronto to Shanghai increases to a daily flight
  • The Toronto to London Heathrow Daytripper returns, part of what Air Canada says will be five daily Toronto flights to the British capital
  • Toronto to Guatemala City, which begins this December, becomes year-round
  • Air Canada Signature (business) class is being added on Bogotá service from both Toronto and Montréal
  • Montréal picks up added frequencies to Palma de Mallorca and Catania in the Mediterranean
  • Montréal to Tenerife, Lima, and Guatemala City all extend to year-round
  • Vancouver to Brisbane expands to daily flights through the summer
  • Halifax to London Heathrow gets lie-flat Signature class and a return to overnight timings

Altogether, Air Canada says it plans to fly more than 125 international routes in summer 2027, reaching over 85 destinations, which it frames as roughly an eight per cent increase in capacity across these regions compared with summer 2026.

My Thoughts

What I find most interesting with this news is not actually one of the new destinations. It is the A321XLR aircraft that Air Canada is using for some of these new routes.

The airline is using this narrowbody to open up smaller European cities like Oslo, Shannon, and Basel. Not only this, the A321XLR will provide a lie-flat business class seat, which is always exciting for travellers who want a premium travel experience even on “smaller” routes. If that seat lives up to the promise, it makes for some appealing shorter transatlantic redemptions to places that were harder to reach before.

A few things to keep in mind. Almost all of these are summer-only, so this is not a year-round map change apart from Guangzhou. The Guangzhou and Oslo routes are still waiting on government approval, so treat those two as tentative until they clear. And while fares are already loaded for cash purchase, you can also purchase these flights with Aeroplan points! As always, be sure to compare points to their cash equivalents to see what the better deal is.

The Bottom Line

This is a big, mostly summer-focused expansion that adds some brand-new non-stops for Canadians, especially out of Toronto and Montréal. The A321XLR routes to Oslo, Shannon, and Basel are the ones I will be watching most closely, and Guangzhou is the standout for anyone heading to southern China.

Which city will you visit first? Whichever one you choose, happy travels!
