Aeroplan Points Expiry Is Back: What You Need to Do Before November 30, 2026

If you hold a balance of Aeroplan points, there is an important change coming.

Starting November 30, 2026, Aeroplan is reinstating its points expiry rule after more than five years on pause. This is not a reset that gives everyone a fresh 18 months. The inactivity clock has been counting the whole time, so if your account already has 18 months or more without any earning or redeeming activity, your points could expire as soon as November 30, 2026. In other words, November 30 is the day points can actually start disappearing, not the day the countdown begins.

Here is what is changing, and the steps you can take to keep your points active.

Header Image Source: Air Canada.

A Quick Bit of Background

Source: Air Canada.

Back in July 2020, in the middle of the pandemic, Air Canada paused the expiry of Aeroplan points. Travel had ground to a halt and most members had no realistic way to earn or redeem, so the clock was frozen. The pause has stayed in place since, and it has already been pushed back once, from November 2025 to November 2026.

That final extension is now up. As of November 30, 2026, the pause ends and the normal expiry policy returns.

How the Expiry Rule Works

Source: Air Canada.

Your Aeroplan account is considered inactive if you go 18 consecutive months without earning or redeeming at least one point. Flying is not required. Any qualifying activity, earning or redeeming, resets the 18-month clock. If the account goes inactive, the points in it are forfeited.

There is also a longer timeline to be aware of. If an account stays inactive for three consecutive years, Air Canada reserves the right to terminate the membership, close the account, and reassign the Aeroplan number.

The Easiest Ways to Stay Active

Source: Air Canada.

You only need one qualifying transaction every 18 months to reset your window. Some of the lowest-effort options:

  • Link Aeroplan to your Uber account and earn points on an Uber or Uber Eats order
  • Add money to your Starbucks account if it is linked to Aeroplan
  • Shop through the Aeroplan eStore, or in store at a participating retail partner
  • Donate a small number of points to charity through Aeroplan
  • Transfer points to or from a friend or family member who is also a member
  • Convert points from another loyalty program into Aeroplan
  • Book a hotel or car rental with points, or redeem for a gift card

Any one of these counts. You do not need to book a flight to keep your balance active.

How to Take Expiry Off the Table Entirely

Source: Air Canada.

Your points will never expire as long as you are the primary cardholder on an eligible Aeroplan credit card from TD, American Express, or CIBC, or if you hold Aeroplan Elite Status.

For members who are already putting everyday spending on a card, an Aeroplan co-branded card does two jobs at once: it earns points on your purchases and it keeps your full balance protected from expiry for as long as you hold it.

A Note for Families

If you use Aeroplan Family Sharing, there is an added layer of protection. When any one family member redeems points, that redemption counts as activity for every family member who has at least one point in their account. That means a single redemption from one person can keep the whole household's points active at the same time.

How Will You Know If Your Points Are at Risk?

Aeroplan will email you up to 60 days before any points are set to expire, so make sure the email address on your account is current. You can also check your status at any time: log in to your Aeroplan account, open the Activity tab, and scroll down to your points inactivity date.

What Happens If My Points Expire

Source: Air Canada.

If your points do expire, there is a six-month window to recover them:

  • Buy them back for a fee
  • Fly with Air Canada within six months of the expiry date. You can book after they expire, but the travel has to happen inside that six-month window
  • Open an eligible Aeroplan credit card

You can also claim missing points from past partner activity, even if you forgot to add your Aeroplan number at the time. That covers hotel stays and car rentals from the last six months, and flights within 11 months of the departure date.

My Thoughts

If you travel regularly or earn through an Aeroplan card, this change likely does not affect you. The members most exposed are those with older accounts that have not been touched since before the pandemic, and family members whose balances have been sitting unused.

In those cases, a single Uber Eats order or a small points transfer is enough to reset the clock. It is worth taking a moment to log in and check your inactivity date.

The Bottom Line

From November 30, 2026, Aeroplan points will start expiring again after 18 months of inactivity, and a three-year lapse puts the entire membership at risk. Log in, check your inactivity date, and make one qualifying transaction if you are close to the line. Holding an Aeroplan card or elite status removes the concern for good.

Happy travels!
