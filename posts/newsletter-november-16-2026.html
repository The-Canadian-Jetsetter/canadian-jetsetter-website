✈️🇨🇦 Flights from $183 + New Route to Japan, Limited-Time Sale

Hey Travellers,

Toronto, and nearby Eastern Canadians, you have a new way to fly to Japan! 🇯🇵✈️

Beginning in March 2027, Japan Airlines’ low-cost carrier, ZIPAIR Tokyo, will start flying from Toronto Pearson to Tokyo Narita. To celebrate, the airline is running a sale on the new route with fares starting at just $479.62 CAD one-way tax inclusive, but it is only live until November 19th, 2026, so do not wait around.

Source: ZIPAIR Tokyo.

You might have heard of ZIPAIR already, since they fly Vancouver to Tokyo and I have featured those deals before. But I find there is a lot of confusion about the airline, which makes sense, because booking a low-cost carrier always comes with a few extra questions. So, see below for some answers!

  1. Is ZIPAIR Tokyo a reputable airline?

    Yes! It is a legitimate airline owned by Japan Airlines, one of Japan’s legacy airlines. The difference between it and its parent company is that ZIPAIR runs based on an à la carte budget model.

  2. Do I get anything with my base ticket, like baggage, meals, etc.?

    Your ticket includes 7 kg of carry-on baggage. Otherwise, as mentioned above, you need to add checked baggage, meals, seat selection, etc. if you so choose. This means if you need these extras, you need to budget an extra $150-$300 per direction for these additional selections, but I find many times, your total price will be much cheaper than booking with a legacy carrier.

  3. Since it is a low-cost carrier, their Economy class legroom must be terrible, right?

    No! Actually, ZIPAIR Tokyo’s Economy class legroom has 31" of seat pitch, which is exactly the same as Air Canada’s.

  4. Does ZIPAIR Tokyo have a business class?

    The airline has something called ZIP Full-Flat, which provides a lie-flat seat in a familiar 1-2-1 seating configuration on their wide-body Boeing 787. Similarly to Economy class though, you need to purchase extras, such as checked baggage, meals, etc., so it is not a traditional business class. You are essentially paying more for a business class-like seat on a long-haul flight, which can be really worth it depending on the price.

  5. Is there seat back entertainment on their aircraft?

    There is no seat back entertainment. Instead, the airline offers high-speed Starlink Wi-Fi, free for all passengers.

So when would I recommend ZIPAIR Tokyo to Canadians, whether you are in Toronto, Vancouver, or can transit through one of these cities?

ZIPAIR Tokyo’s Economy Class and ZIP Full-Flat “Business Class.” Source: ZIPAIR Tokyo.

If you travel light, this is the perfect airline for you. And if you do want extras like checked baggage and onboard meals, add them up first: if your total still comes in under Air Canada, JAL, and the rest, then I would absolutely say book with ZIPAIR.

One more thing worth noting: more competition means lower prices across the board, since airlines have to fight to win you over. So this is a great development for Toronto, and for Canadians in general. Here is hoping ZIPAIR Tokyo, and other carriers, keep adding more direct routes to Japan!
