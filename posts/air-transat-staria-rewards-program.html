Air Transat Launches Staria: A Breakdown of the New Canadian Rewards Program

Air Transat has finally entered the loyalty game. On November 2, 2026, the airline launched Staria, its new rewards program, and it is open for enrolment right now. For an airline that so many Canadians already fly to the sun and across to Europe, a proper rewards program has been a long time coming.

The good news is that Staria is refreshingly simple. There is no sprawling award chart to decode and no status tiers to chase. The pitch from Air Transat is that you do not need to be a frequent flyer to get something out of it, and after digging through the details, I think that pitch mostly holds up. Let me break down how it actually works and whether it is worth your time.

The Three Pieces: Points, Stars, and Constellations

Source: Air Transat.

Staria is built on three ideas, and understanding all three is the key to understanding the program.

Points are the currency. You earn them when you book and you spend them to knock money off a future trip. Stars are a separate gamified layer that you collect by completing small tasks, and they unlock travel perks. Constellations are group accounts that let you pool points with family or friends. Most programs give you one of these. Staria is trying to do all three at once, which is ambitious for a day-one launch.

How You Earn Staria Points

Source: Air Transat.

Earning is tied to how you book, and the rate climbs with the fare you choose. On flights operated by Air Transat or Porter Airlines, the fare class decides your rate. Here is how it all breaks down:

How you earn Staria points
Purchase Fare or product Points per $1 CAD
Flights operated by Air Transat or Porter Eco Budget 3
Flights operated by Air Transat or Porter Eco Standard, Club Standard 5
Flights operated by Air Transat or Porter Eco Flex, Club Flex 7
Transat vacation packages Flight, hotel, transfers 1
Air Transat options and extras Seats, bags, meals, Option Plus 5

Earning is calculated on the base fare before taxes and airport fees, and the usual suspects are excluded: taxes, surcharges, car rentals, excursions, insurance, and donations do not earn. Add-ons like seat selection, checked bags, and pre-ordered meals only earn when you add them at the time of booking.

The detail worth flagging here is Porter. Having Porter flights earn Staria points out of the gate is a smart move, and it quietly widens where you can rack up a balance beyond Air Transat's own leisure network.

One more nice touch: when you pay for a trip partly in points and partly in cash, you still earn points on the cash portion. You are not penalized for redeeming.

How You Redeem, and What a Point Is Actually Worth

Source: Air Transat.

This is where Staria breaks from the programs most of our readers know. There is no fixed award chart. Instead, the number of points you need is dynamic and tracks the cash price of the ticket, and you decide how much of the fare to cover, anywhere from a minimum of 10% all the way up to 100% on Air Transat flights. On Transat packages, points can cover up to 40% of the total.

So what is a point worth? So far, Air Transat has not published an official value, but from pricing out real itineraries on their website, a point appears to be worth approximately 1 cent CAD each, and testing across different redemption amounts returned right around 1 cent per point on every option.

Take a one-way Montreal to Lisbon flight in Eco Budget as an example. The cash price comes to $865.41 CAD all in, which breaks down to $390.00 in base fare, $395.00 in carrier surcharge, and $80.41 in airport taxes. Because points cannot be applied to taxes and airport fees, the most they can cover is the $785.00 fare portion. Here is how the redemption tiers priced out:

  • 7,300 points saved $73.00, or about 1 cent per point
  • 14,400 points saved $145.00, or about 1 cent per point
  • 21,700 points saved $218.00, or about 1 cent per point
  • 28,900 points saved $290.00, or about 1 cent per point
  • 78,500 points saved the full $785.00 fare, or about 1 cent per point

Every tier lands on the same number. You are always looking at roughly a penny of value per point, and that $80.41 in airport taxes stays as a cash charge no matter how many points you throw at the booking.

That consistency is the most important thing to understand about this program. One cent per point makes Staria a fixed-value, rebate-style program rather than a traditional miles program. To put it in context, we generally value Aeroplan points at around 2 cents each, so a Staria point is worth roughly half of an Aeroplan point. The trade-off is that you give up the outsized value a sweet-spot Aeroplan redemption can deliver, but you gain total predictability. There are no blackout dates, no capacity controls, and no award availability to hunt for. If there is a seat for sale, you can put points toward it.

For a leisure traveller who flies Air Transat once or twice a year, that simplicity is useful. You will not be squeezing premium-cabin value out of these points, but you also will not be fighting the program to use them.

Stars: The Perks Layer

Source: Air Transat.

Stars are the part of Staria that feels new. Rather than earning them by spending, you collect stars by completing what Air Transat calls missions. These are small tasks like completing your profile, filling out a survey, subscribing to the newsletter, joining a constellation, referring a friend, or simply logging into your account once a month.

Accumulate enough stars and you unlock a choice of rewards. The launch lineup includes a free first checked bag, a free pre-ordered Bistro meal, free seat selection, priority boarding, and bonus points, with more promised over time. Once a reward choice unlocks, you have 30 days to pick one, otherwise a default reward is assigned automatically.

It is a light touch of gamification, and the smart angle is that it rewards engagement between trips, not just at checkout. For an occasional flyer, chipping away at a free checked bag without spending a dollar is a reasonable perk to have.

Constellations: Pooling Points With Your People

Source: Air Transat.

The constellation feature is the one I expect families to care about most. You can group up with up to 8 other members, for a total of 9 people, into a single shared balance. Every member's points flow into that shared pool once their trips are completed, which means a family can reach a free or discounted trip far faster than any one person could alone.

The mechanics are handled fairly. When points are redeemed from the pool, each member's contribution is adjusted proportionally, so no one is subsidizing everyone else. If you leave a constellation, you recover your share of the points minus anything already redeemed. The one catch to know: there is a mandatory 6-month waiting period before you can join a new constellation after leaving one, so this is not something to shuffle in and out of.

Signing Up, and What Is Coming Next

Source: Air Transat.

Joining is free and open to anyone (well, anyone over the age of two!), with kids enrolled by a parent or guardian. You sign up by verifying your email and filling in your details, and you will want to add your membership number at the time of booking to make sure your points post. If you forget, you can file a missing points claim within 6 months of your trip.

The bigger piece is still on the horizon. Desjardins and Visa are Staria's strategic partners, with co-branded credit cards that earn points on everyday spending set to launch later in 2027. That is the development to watch. A program that only earns when you fly is limited, but a credit card that earns Staria points on groceries and gas changes the math entirely, and it is how a rebate-style program like this becomes truly worth collecting.

My Thoughts

Staria is not going to replace Aeroplan in your wallet, and it is not trying to. What it is, is a clean, low-friction program for the many Canadians who were already flying Air Transat and getting nothing back for it. The three-part structure of points, stars, and constellations is more thoughtful than I expected from a launch, and the Porter earning partnership plus the family pooling are the standout features.

The honest limitation is the value. At roughly 1 cent per point, you should think of Staria as a modest rebate on money you were going to spend anyway, not as a way to unlock aspirational travel. There are also no transfer partners and no way to buy or move points outside of a constellation, so the program is self-contained for now. The real test comes in 2027 with the Desjardins and Visa cards.

The Bottom Line

If you fly Air Transat or Porter even occasionally, there is no reason not to sign up. It is free, it takes a few minutes, and you start earning on your very next booking and on perks you can collect without spending. Just go in with the right expectation: this is a simple, predictable rebate program, and the most interesting chapter is still a year away.

Happy travels!
