Air Canada Adds Premium Economy Basic and Business Class Basic Fares in North America

If you book premium economy or business class with Air Canada on a flight within Canada or down to the United States, there is a new fare option worth knowing about.

As of November 17, 2026, the airline has added Premium Economy Basic and Business Class Basic to its North American network. Both sit at the bottom of their cabin, priced below the Standard fare, and both trade away a handful of the extras you would normally get up front in return for a lower starting price.

This is not the first time these fares have surfaced. Air Canada first rolled them out back in July on a set of international markets, and North America is the latest region to join the list. The move matters because these are the routes where most Canadians actually fly, so a lot more travellers will now run into these fares the next time they shop for a premium seat.

Header Image Source: Air Canada.

What Is Included, and What Is Left Out

Source: Air Canada.

The simplest way to think about a Basic fare is that you keep the seat and the in-flight experience, but you give up some of the flexibility and a few of the perks that come bundled into a Standard or Latitude fare. Here is how all three fares line up in each cabin, side by side.

A couple of things stand out once you see them lined up. In premium economy, Maple Leaf Lounge access is never free: it is not offered at all on Basic, and it costs extra even on Standard and Latitude, so you only get in at no charge if you have status or an eligible Aeroplan card. Business class is more generous. If you are in Business Class Basic, the Maple Leaf Lounge and Air Canada Café are still yours (just not the Signature Suite), and the sports equipment handling fee is waived across all three business fares. Neither perk carries over to premium economy.

Across both cabins, the Basic trade-off is the same. You lose refundability, free seat selection, the second checked bag, and eUpgrade eligibility, but the seat, the onboard meal, the priority perks, and full Aeroplan earning all stay exactly where they are.

How the Prices Actually Compare

A lower fare only means something if the gap is real, so I pulled up a non-stop Montreal to Calgary flight for December 11, 2026 to see how the fares line up side by side.

Source: Air Canada.

Here is premium economy, one-way:

  • Premium Economy Basic: $799 CAD
  • Premium Economy Standard: $856 CAD
  • Premium Economy Latitude: $1,098 CAD

Source: Air Canada.

And here is business class on the same flight:

  • Business Class Basic: $1,104 CAD
  • Business Class Standard: $1,184 CAD
  • Business Class Latitude: $1,541 CAD

The thing that jumps out is how narrow the gap is. Basic undercuts Standard by about $57 CAD in premium economy and roughly $80 CAD in business class. For that difference, Standard hands you free seat selection, a second checked bag, and eUpgrade eligibility, plus the option to cancel for a travel credit. If you were going to pay to pick a seat or check a second bag anyway, Standard quietly becomes the better deal.

That said, the gap is not fixed. It varies by route and date, and it tends to widen on longer international flights where the fares have been sold since the summer. It is always worth pulling both fares up side by side before you commit.

You Still Earn Full SQC and Aeroplan Points

Source: Air Canada.

Here is the part that makes these fares more interesting than they look at first glance. Even though Basic is the cheapest premium fare, it still earns Status Qualifying Credits at the full premium rate of 4 SQC per dollar on the base fare and carrier surcharges. That is the same rate Standard and Latitude earn.

This is a big contrast with economy, where Basic fares earn zero SQC and you have to climb to Flex before you hit that same 4 SQC per dollar. On the premium side, Air Canada has not clawed back any earning. You get the lowest price in the cabin and the full status credits that go with a premium ticket. Aeroplan points are earned on the same base, so nothing changes there either.

For anyone chasing status, that combination is the selling point. A Basic fare lets you sit up front, pay less than Standard, and still earn toward 25K and beyond at the full clip.

Status and Credit Card Benefits Carry Over

Source: Air Canada.

Booking a Basic fare does not strip away your Aeroplan Elite Status benefits. The Elite points multiplier, priority services, status-based lounge access, and complimentary checked baggage all still apply. For Elite members, that complimentary baggage is a nice cushion, since it can quietly replace the second checked bag that Basic leaves out.

Benefits from eligible Aeroplan co-branded credit cards also carry over, which narrows the gap between Basic and Standard even further if you already hold one of those cards.

The one real exception is upgrades. You cannot use eUpgrades on either Basic fare, and Priority Rewards vouchers are off the table too. If an upgrade is part of your plan, Standard is the lowest fare that keeps you eligible.

Where You Can Book Them

The new Basic fares are available on flights within Canada and between Canada and the United States. They join the international markets where Air Canada introduced them back in July, which cover the Caribbean, Mexico, Latin America, and South America, along with transatlantic joint venture routes flown alongside United and Lufthansa Group.

Note that flights to Asia and the South Pacific continue to sell the usual premium fares for now.

My Thoughts

As far as fare unbundling goes, this is a fairly gentle version of it. The seat and the onboard experience stay intact, and the full SQC earning means Air Canada is not punishing you for choosing the cheaper option the way it does down in economy.

The catch is that the savings are slim on the North American routes I looked at. When Basic is only $57 CAD below Standard, the math almost always favours Standard once you factor in a seat assignment or a second bag. Where Basic earns its keep is the traveller who packs light, has firm plans, and does not care where they sit.

The Bottom Line

Air Canada has brought its Basic premium fares to the routes most Canadians actually fly. They lower the entry price into premium economy and business class while keeping the seat, the meal, the priority perks, and full status earning. You give up refundability, free seat selection, a second bag, and eUpgrades to get there.

On short domestic hops the gap to Standard is small enough that it is worth running the numbers both ways, but if you travel light and lock in your plans early, a Basic premium fare is something you can now book.

Happy travels!
