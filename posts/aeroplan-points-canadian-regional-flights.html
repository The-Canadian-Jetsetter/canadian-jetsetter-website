Why Aeroplan Points Crush Cash on Canada's Most Expensive Regional Flights

Air travel within Canada, particularly to and from northern and remote communities, is among the most expensive in the developed world. For the residents of places like Yellowknife, Churchill, and Gaspé, flying is not a luxury. It is often the only practical way to reach medical appointments, family, and essential services, and the cash fares on these thin regional routes routinely climb well past what a comparable flight to Europe would cost. For prospective visitors, those same prices can be enough to put an entire region out of reach.

The result is a real barrier to travel. When a short domestic flight costs several hundred, or even close to a thousand dollars, it limits who can visit these communities and, in many cases, how easily the people who live there can leave them.

Points and miles will not fix the underlying economics of regional aviation, but they can meaningfully soften the blow. One of the most effective tools available to Canadian travellers is booking these routes through Aeroplan, where a short regional flight reliably prices at a low, predictable number of points no matter how punishing the cash fare has become.

How the Pricing Actually Works

Source: Aeroplan

Aeroplan is one of the very few major loyalty programs that still publishes an award chart, so you are rarely booking blind. The important thing to understand is that not every flight is priced the same way, and knowing the difference is what unlocks the value.

Flights operated by Air Canada itself are fully dynamic. The points price moves with demand, just like a cash fare, so it can swing dramatically from one date to the next. That is the pricing most people are used to, and it is often mediocre value.

Flights operated by Air Canada's partner airlines behave very differently. Most Aeroplan partner airlines have fixed pricing, meaning no matter what the cash price is, the redemption value for their routes will remain the same. On the regional and cross-border partners in this article though (Canadian North, PAL Airlines, Bearskin Airlines, United Airlines, and Calm Air), Aeroplan technically classifies them as dynamically priced, but in practice the number has settled into a stable, predictable rate, and for short economy hops that rate reliably lands at 15,000 points each way. It does not chase the cash fare upward. Whether the cash ticket is $400 CAD or nearly $1,000 CAD, the partner award holds steady at 15,000 points.

Two more things are worth knowing. First, a partner booking fee plus taxes still apply, and that is the cash portion you will see listed beside each redemption below. Second, when Aeroplan adjusted its award chart in 2026, everything within the North America zone was left untouched, so this pricing is very much alive today. It is not a hard guarantee, and a high-demand date can nudge the partner price higher, so it is always worth checking, but on these routes 15,000 points is what you will see the vast majority of the time.

Below are five examples, each a stronger value than the last.

1. Edmonton to Yellowknife with Canadian North

Getting to the North has never been cheap. This nonstop on Canadian North rings in at $400.52 CAD one-way in cash, or 15,000 Aeroplan points plus $91 CAD in taxes and fees.

That is a value of 2.06 cents per point (CAD), which comfortably beats the roughly 1.5-2 cents most people should treat as their baseline for an Aeroplan point. A strong start, and a route where cash prices rarely dip.

2. Quebec City to Gaspé with PAL Airlines

This short run out to the Gaspé Peninsula on PAL Airlines is the kind of regional route that punishes cash buyers. The fare sits at $494.91 CAD one-way, versus 15,000 points plus $96 CAD in fees.

That works out to 2.66 cents per point (CAD) for a flight that is barely an hour and a half in the air.

3. Winnipeg to Thunder Bay with Bearskin Airlines

A one-way ticket from Winnipeg to Thunder Bay on Bearskin Airlines will set you back a staggering $720.70 CAD in cash. The exact same seat is 15,000 points plus $89 CAD in fees.

That is a value of 4.21 cents per point (CAD). Paying over $700 CAD for a short regional flight is rough. Paying 15,000 points is a completely different story.

4. Calgary to Denver with United Airlines

The value is not limited to flights inside Canada. It works the same way across the border, and United Airlines routes can produce some of the best returns of all.

Calgary to Denver came in at $816 CAD one-way in cash, or 15,000 points plus $158 CAD in fees. That is 4.39 cents per point (CAD).

One important tip on this one. United tends to jack up its cash fares dramatically in the days leading up to departure, while the Aeroplan partner price does not follow it up. That means a last-minute booking is where you will squeeze the most value out of this move, exactly when a cash ticket would hurt the most.

5. Winnipeg to Churchill with Calm Air

And here is the one that makes the whole case. Getting to Churchill, the polar bear capital of the world, is famously expensive. This nonstop on Calm Air costs an eye-watering $966.45 CAD one-way in cash.

Book it with Aeroplan and you pay 15,000 points plus $79 CAD in fees. That is a value of 5.91 cents per point (CAD), turning a near four-figure flight into one of the best point redemptions you will find anywhere in the country.

My Thoughts

With any loyalty program, the real skill is not earning the points. It is knowing how to squeeze the most value out of them. Aeroplan is a great example, because the stable partner pricing on these routes creates a handful of standout redemptions where the value is difficult to beat. Many of them are on exactly the kind of expensive northern and regional routes shown above, where using points instead of cash can save hundreds of dollars per person on a single one-way ticket.

The key is to always compare the cash price to the points price before you book. The partner pricing shines when the cash fare is high, which is precisely the situation on these thin regional routes. It makes far less sense when a flight is already cheap and could be bought outright for less than your points are worth. That is why 15,000 points is a useful yardstick. When a short domestic flight is priced at several hundred dollars or more, as in every example above, the points option is almost always the smarter play. When the cash fare is low, keep your points and pay cash.

It also pays to remember the distinction from earlier. If the only option on your route is an Air Canada-operated flight, the price is dynamic and can be far less predictable, so it is worth searching specifically for a partner-operated flight where you can. That is where the reliable 15,000 point pricing lives.

The Bottom Line

Cash fares on short domestic and regional flights in Canada are punishing, and they are not getting better. Booking these routes through Aeroplan on a partner airline is one of the few tools that consistently beats them, holding at a reliable 15,000 points on short-haul economy no matter what the cash price does. If you are sitting on a stack of Aeroplan points, these are some of the smartest ways to spend them.

Happy travels!
