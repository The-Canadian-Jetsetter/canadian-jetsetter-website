Cathay Pacific Turns 80: Up To $268 CAD Off Flights To Asia From Toronto And Vancouver

Cathay Pacific is turning 80, and to mark the occasion the airline is running an anniversary sale on round-trip flights out of Toronto and Vancouver. The headline number is up to $268 CAD off, but there is a bit more going on under the hood: bonus Asia Miles, a discount code for your next flight, and a Flight and Hotel offer if you are pairing a stay with your trip.

The catch, as always with these things, is that the best codes are capped at a small number of tickets and everything runs on a first-come, first-served basis. So if any of this is on your radar, it is worth moving quickly.

All of these promotions end September 22, 2026.

Header Image Source: Cathay Pacific

How The Discount Works

Source: Cathay Pacific.

There are four promotion codes, and which one you use depends on your cabin and where you are flying. Here is the breakdown:

  • CX80CABUS: $268 CAD off Business class to Hong Kong and beyond, limited to the first 80 tickets.
  • CX80CAPEY: $238 CAD off Premium Economy to Hong Kong and beyond, limited to the first 80 tickets.
  • CX80CA: $138 CAD off Economy, Premium Economy, or Business to everywhere except Hong Kong, limited to the first 1,300 tickets.
  • CX80HKCA: $128 CAD off Economy, Premium Economy, or Business to Hong Kong only, limited to the first 700 tickets.

The practical takeaway: if you are flying Business, always reach for CX80CABUS ($268 CAD off), and if you are flying Premium Economy, use CX80CAPEY ($238 CAD off), whether your destination is Hong Kong or somewhere beyond it. For Economy, it comes down to your destination, so use CX80CA for anywhere beyond Hong Kong and CX80HKCA if Hong Kong itself is the goal.

A few booking notes worth knowing before you dive in. These codes are available only to Cathay members, though membership is free, so you just need to sign up and be signed in when you search. The booking has to be round-trip and made on the Cathay Pacific Canada website rather than the mobile app, departing from Vancouver (YVR) or Toronto (YYZ) between September 10 and December 10, 2026. Enter your code in the promotion code field at the search stage, because it will not be accepted once you reach checkout.

The Bonus Asia Miles

Source: Cathay Pacific.

On top of the fare discount, Cathay is handing out up to 1,946 bonus Asia Miles with a booking:

  • 1,080 bonus miles for new Cathay members. You can sign up here if you are not already a member.
  • An additional 866 bonus miles if your Cathay membership number happens to contain an 8 or a 0.

It is a fun little anniversary touch, and if you are booking anyway, there is no reason to leave those miles on the table.

28% Off Your Next Flight

Here is the piece I think is easy to overlook. After you make your first booking, you can grab 28% off your next eligible round-trip Economy flight, though this one is limited to the first 100 customers only.

That next-flight discount is valid for departures between January 8 and May 23, 2027, so it is a nice way to lock in a second trip early next year while the value is still there.

Bonus For Package Travellers

If you would rather bundle a hotel in, there is a separate Cathay Holidays offer: $310 CAD off Flight and Hotel packages to Hong Kong for two or more travellers, using code CX80WTRP6J, bookable by September 22, 2026, with added perks at selected hotels.

Seeing It In Action

Source: Cathay Pacific.

Discount codes are only as good as the fares they sit on top of, so I ran three real examples, one in each cabin, to show what this actually looks like. In each case I started on Google Flights to establish the baseline fare, then moved over to Cathay's own site to apply the code and confirm the final price.

Example 1: Economy, Vancouver to Manila

First up, a straightforward Economy run from Vancouver to Manila, departing September 17 and returning September 30. I started on Google Flights to get my bearings, where this round trip was showing at around $1,003 CAD.

Over on Cathay's own site, the same Economy Light itinerary priced out at $1,009.97 CAD before any code. One quick note here: the number on Google can sit a touch lower than Cathay's direct fare because Google also pulls in third-party sellers, so I always treat the airline's own site as the true baseline. From there I entered CX80CA in the promotion code field, and the $138 CAD discount came straight off the top.

That took the round trip from $1,009.97 CAD down to $871.97 CAD, a clean $138 CAD off an already reasonable Economy fare to the Philippines.

It is worth putting that in context. Even before the code, Cathay was already the cheapest option on this route, and after the discount it lands almost $300 CAD below China Airlines, the next-cheapest carrier at $1,170 CAD.

Example 2: Premium Economy, Vancouver to Jakarta

Next, a step up into Premium Economy, Vancouver to Jakarta, departing September 28 and returning October 12. Google had this one at about $2,189 CAD once third-party sellers were factored in, with Cathay's own booking option listed at $2,303 CAD.

On Cathay's site, the Premium Economy Essential fare came to $2,302.87 CAD before any code. One thing worth flagging on this route: it is a mixed-cabin itinerary. The long-haul legs between Vancouver and Hong Kong are in Premium Economy, but the shorter regional hop between Hong Kong and Jakarta is in Economy, with the fare conditions still following Premium Economy. That comes straight from one of the offer's footnotes: Premium Economy is not sold on every route, and where it is not available you are seated in Economy for that leg without a refund of the difference. It is a good thing to check depending on exactly where you are headed. Applying CX80CAPEY took $238 CAD off the fare.

That dropped the round trip from $2,302.87 CAD to $2,064.87 CAD, a $238 CAD saving in Premium Economy.

For a bit of context, the next-cheapest option here was a United and Singapore Airlines itinerary at $3,187 CAD, and that one routed through two stops rather than one. Set against the discounted fare, that is a saving of just over $1,122 CAD, on a simpler routing.

Example 3: Business, Toronto to Beijing

Finally, the one I suspect most of you came here for: Business class. This is Toronto to Beijing, departing September 21 and returning October 5. Google Flights had Cathay Business fares on this route sitting in the $5,400 to $5,500 CAD range.

The exact itinerary I ended up pricing ran through Cathay's own site at $5,416.62 CAD before any code, on a cleaner routing than Google's cheapest option. As with the Jakarta example this is a mixed-cabin itinerary, and there is one added wrinkle worth knowing about: the regional Hong Kong to Beijing leg is operated by Air China and seated in Economy, though the fare conditions still follow Business. In plain terms, you get the full Cathay Business experience on the long-haul legs across the Pacific, with a partner-operated Economy hop at the far end. With CX80CABUS applied, the full $268 CAD discount comes off.

That brought the round trip from $5,416.62 CAD down to $5,148.62 CAD, a $268 CAD saving on a lie-flat Business seat across the Pacific.

This one is a little different. An Air Canada and Korean Air itinerary was actually cheaper to start with, at $5,184 CAD, but it routed through two stops. Compared with the discounted price of $5,148.62 CAD, you come out around $35 CAD ahead and have just one layover to deal with instead of two.

My Thoughts

I want to be straight about this one. In percentage terms these are not enormous discounts. On the Economy side, $138 CAD off a roughly $1,000 CAD fare is a nice trim but not a reason on its own to book a trip you were not already planning, and even in Business, $268 CAD off a fare north of $5,000 CAD is a small slice proportionally.

Where the offer gets more interesting is when you stack everything together. A flat dollar discount, the bonus Asia Miles, and 28% off your next flight all layered onto a product that is consistently rated among the world's best add up to a package that is easy to say yes to, provided you were already going to book Cathay in the first place.

And the three examples show the discount doing more than trimming a few dollars. In Economy it pushed an already-cheapest fare to around $300 CAD below the next airline, in Premium Economy it opened a gap of more than $1,000 CAD against the nearest alternative, and even in Business, where the raw saving is smallest, it edged out a cheaper competitor while cutting out a connection. On these routes you are often getting the best price, the simpler routing, and the better onboard product all at once.

The thing to watch is the quotas. The Business and Premium Economy codes are each capped at just 80 tickets, and once they are gone, they are gone. The Economy codes have far more headroom, but even those are finite. Pair that with a booking deadline of September 22 and a fairly tight travel window through December 10, and this becomes a move-quickly situation for anyone whose plans already line up.

The Bottom Line

If you were already eyeing a fall trip to Asia and you can fly out of Toronto or Vancouver, this is easy value to grab, especially if you are booking Business or Premium Economy where the discounts are largest and the bonus miles sweeten the deal further. Just do not talk yourself into a trip purely for the discount, and if the premium-cabin codes are what you are after, treat that 80-ticket cap as a real deadline and book sooner rather than later.

One quick note: these codes run on a first-come, first-served basis and are subject to availability, and cash fares can shift at any time. Confirm the final price before you pay.

Happy travels!
