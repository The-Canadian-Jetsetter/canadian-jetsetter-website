ZIPAIR Tokyo Is Coming to Toronto: New Nonstop Flights to Japan in 2027

Toronto is about to get a brand new way to reach Japan, and it comes from an airline that has been quietly shaking up long haul travel out of Canada.

ZIPAIR Tokyo, the low cost carrier owned by Japan Airlines, is adding year round nonstop service between Toronto and Tokyo Narita in 2027. It is a significant addition for Eastern Canada, and it makes Toronto only the second Canadian city ZIPAIR serves, after the airline launched in Vancouver back in 2024.

Here is what the new route looks like, what ZIPAIR actually is, and what you can expect on board.

Header Image Source: ZIPAIR Tokyo.

A New Nonstop Route: Toronto to Tokyo

Service begins in March 2027, with ZIPAIR flying the route three times per week on its Boeing 787 Dreamliner. From April 2027, that steps up to four times per week. Unlike the seasonal routes that come and go, this is year round service, so it will be an option no matter when you want to travel.

Flights land at Tokyo Narita, which sits roughly 60 kilometres east of central Tokyo. Narita is well connected to the city by train, so the extra distance compared to Haneda is quite easy to manage.

Right now, Air Canada is the only airline offering nonstop flights between Toronto and Tokyo, and those fares can climb steeply depending on the season. The stretch from late March into early April tends to be the most expensive of all, since that is peak cherry blossom season and everyone wants to be in Japan.

Adding a second nonstop carrier on this route is exactly the kind of competition that tends to push fares down, which is great news for anyone in Toronto, or anyone able to connect through it.

So What Exactly Is ZIPAIR Tokyo?

Source: ZIPAIR Tokyo.

If the name is new to you, here is the short version: ZIPAIR is a legitimate, well backed airline. It was founded in 2018 and is wholly owned by Japan Airlines.

The airline flies a fleet of Boeing 787-8 Dreamliners, the same modern long haul aircraft you will find at plenty of full service airlines. Its network already spans Asia and North America, with destinations including Seoul, Bangkok, Los Angeles, San Francisco, Houston, Orlando, and of course Vancouver. Toronto now joins that list.

The key difference between ZIPAIR and its parent company is the business model. Where a traditional airline bundles everything into one fare, ZIPAIR runs on an à la carte model: the base fare is kept low, and you add only the extras you actually want.

What You Get, and What Costs Extra

Source: ZIPAIR Tokyo.

Flying a low cost carrier across the Pacific comes with a few more decisions than booking Air Canada or JAL, so it helps to know what is included before you book.

Your base Economy fare includes two carry on items totalling up to 7 kg. Just about everything else is optional and priced separately, including checked baggage, meals, and seat selection. If you need those extras, budget for them up front, but in many cases the all in total still lands below what a full service airline would charge.

A few things worth knowing about the onboard experience:

  • Legroom: Economy offers 31 inches of seat pitch, which is right in line with most international carriers and matches what Air Canada offers in its Economy cabin.
  • Wi-Fi: ZIPAIR offers complimentary high speed internet for everyone on board, which is still rare on long haul flights.
  • Entertainment: There are no seat back screens, so you stream free entertainment to your own device instead. Pack a tablet or a loaded up phone and a good pair of headphones.
  • Food and drink: You can purchase snacks, drinks, alcoholic beverages, and even duty free products on board.

ZIP Full-Flat: A Lie-Flat Seat Without the Premium Price

Source: ZIPAIR Tokyo.

ZIPAIR’s most interesting feature sits at the front of the aircraft. Its ZIP Full-Flat product offers a lie flat seat in a familiar 1-2-1 configuration on the Boeing 787, the same layout you would find in business class on a major airline.

The catch, in keeping with the à la carte approach, is that it does not come with the full suite of traditional business class service. You are paying for a flat bed to sleep on, not for champagne and multi course meals. For travellers who care most about arriving rested, that can be a tremendous value, and on the Vancouver route I have seen Full-Flat seats priced below what premium economy costs on other airlines.

A Real Toronto to Tokyo Price Comparison

Source: ZIPAIR Tokyo.

Numbers make the case better than I can, so here is a real round trip on the same set of dates, June 10 to 28, 2027, with ZIPAIR lined up against Air Canada in both Economy and business class.

Economy

ZIPAIR keeps the base Economy fare low, at around $900 CAD round trip, and lets you add one of three optional packages in each direction. Here is what each one includes:

  • Value ($161.00 CAD outbound, $145.39 CAD inbound): advance seat selection, a standard in-flight meal, and one checked bag of up to 30 kg.
  • Premium ($238.00 CAD outbound, $215.74 CAD inbound): everything in Value, plus an amenity set and an added carry-on piece, for 15 kg of carry-on in total.
  • Flex Biz ($150.00 CAD outbound, $136.01 CAD inbound): advance seat selection, the larger 15 kg carry-on allowance, and ticket change options, though no checked bag or meal.

For this comparison I have used the Value package in each direction, which adds $161.00 CAD outbound and $145.39 CAD inbound. All in, the total lands at $1,205.86 CAD. See the ZIPAIR fare →

My Thoughts

This is a great development for Toronto, and for Canadian travellers in general. More competition on a route almost always works in your favour, because airlines have to fight harder to win your booking. Toronto travellers have had just one nonstop option to Tokyo for a long time, and adding a second carrier, especially a value focused one, should help keep fares honest across the board.

ZIPAIR will not be the right fit for everyone. If you want lounge access, checked bags included, and a full meal service, a traditional carrier is still your airline. But if you travel light, or you are happy to add a bag and a meal and still come out ahead, it is hard to argue with the value.

My advice is simple: add up the extras you actually need, compare the all in price to its competitors, and book whichever comes out ahead. Often enough, that will be ZIPAIR.

The Bottom Line

Starting in March 2027, Toronto will have a brand new nonstop link to Tokyo on modern 787 Dreamliners, year round. It is more choice, more competition, and very likely lower fares on one of the most popular long haul routes out of Canada.

Whether ZIPAIR becomes your go to depends on how you like to travel, but having the option on the table is a win for everyone flying between Eastern Canada and Japan.
